    Study 11: Smoke Ascending Forever -- Advanced Study¶

    Revelation 14:9-11 and 19:3 -- OT Source Imagery, Greek Vocabulary, and Genre Analysis

    Evidence Tiers

    This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:

    1. Explicit (E) -- The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
    2. Necessary Implication (N) -- No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed -- every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
    3. Inference (I) -- Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be added beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.

    The hierarchy matters: Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference. An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

    Section A: The OT Fire-and-Brimstone Paradigm¶

    1. Trace the fire-and-brimstone paradigm across the OT. For each passage, identify the subject and the result:

      Passage Subject Result (what happens to the subject?)
      Genesis 19:24-25 Sodom and Gomorrah ___
      Deuteronomy 29:23 The land under God's curse ___
      Psalm 11:6 The wicked ___
      Isaiah 30:33 Tophet (prepared for the king) ___
      Isaiah 34:9-10 Edom ___
      Ezekiel 38:22 Gog's armies ___

      In any of these passages, does the fire-and-brimstone produce ongoing conscious torment? Or does it produce destruction and desolation in every verifiable case?

    2. The Hebrew word for brimstone is gophriyth (H1614). It occurs 7 times in the OT. Read Genesis 19:24, Deuteronomy 29:23, Job 18:15, Psalm 11:6, Isaiah 30:33, Isaiah 34:9, and Ezekiel 38:22. In how many of these occurrences does brimstone function as an agent of destructive judgment? In how many does it describe ongoing conscious torment?

    3. Read Luke 17:29. This is the only non-Revelation NT use of theion (G2303, brimstone). Jesus says fire and brimstone "destroyed them all." What does Jesus' own description of the fire-and-brimstone paradigm tell you about its meaning?

    4. Read Ezekiel 38:22 alongside Ezekiel 39:11-12. After the fire-and-brimstone judgment on Gog (38:22), what does Ezek 39:11-12 describe? What does seven months of burying corpses tell you about whether the brimstone judgment produces ongoing torment or death?

    Section B: Isaiah 34 -- The Source Text for "Smoke Ascending Forever"¶

    1. Read Isaiah 34:9-10 carefully. The text uses multiple durational expressions: "not quenched night nor day," "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever," and "from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever." The last phrase uses the strongest available "forever" form in Hebrew (le-netsach netsachim). Now read Isaiah 34:11-17. After the strongest "forever" language in the OT, animals permanently inhabit the territory. What does this demonstrate about how the "forever fire/smoke" formula functions?

    2. Isaiah 34:9 uses gophriyth (brimstone) -- the same word as Genesis 19:24 (Sodom). What does this shared vocabulary tell you about the relationship between the Edom and Sodom judgment paradigms?

    3. Read Isaiah 1:31. "The strong shall be as tow, and the maker of it as a spark, and they shall both burn together, and none shall quench them." The phrase "none shall quench" parallels Isaiah 34:10 ("not quenched"). Tow (flax fiber) is fully consumed when burned. What does "unquenchable fire" describe -- a fire that never stops, or a fire that cannot be stopped until it finishes its work?

    Section C: Greek Vocabulary in Revelation 14¶

    1. The Greek word basanismos (G929, "torment/torturing") occurs 6 times in the NT -- all in Revelation. Document each occurrence:

      Reference Subject Context
      Rev 9:5 ___ ___
      Rev 14:11 ___ ___
      Rev 18:7 ___ ___
      Rev 18:10 ___ ___
      Rev 18:15 ___ ___

      How many of the 6 occurrences describe Babylon's "torment"? What IS Babylon's torment -- is she still being tormented, or was she utterly destroyed (Rev 18:8,21)?

    2. Rev 9:5 uses basanismos with an explicit time limit of five months. What does this tell you about whether basanismos inherently means "eternal"?

    3. The verb basanizo (G928, "to torment/test") has a wide semantic range. Document the following non-judgment uses:

      Reference Subject Meaning
      Matthew 8:6 ___ ___
      Matthew 14:24 ___ ___
      Mark 6:48 ___ ___
      2 Peter 2:8 ___ ___
      Revelation 12:2 ___ ___

      Does the word inherently mean "eternal conscious suffering," or does it describe various forms of distress, testing, and difficulty?

    4. The Greek word anapausis (G372, "rest/cessation") appears in both Rev 14:11 ("they have no rest day nor night") and Rev 4:8 ("they rest not day and night"). Same word, same book, same author. In Rev 4:8, the living creatures' unceasing praise is described with the same "no rest day and night" formula. What does the Rev 4:8 usage demonstrate about whether "no rest day and night" describes eternal duration or the continuous quality of an activity during its execution?

    5. Read Revelation 14:13, two verses after 14:11. The faithful dead "rest" (anapauomai, a cognate of anapausis) from their labors. What does the juxtaposition of "no rest" for beast-worshippers (14:11) and "rest" for the faithful dead (14:13) suggest about the literary structure of this passage?

    Section D: Genre Gate Analysis -- Apocalyptic Vision¶

    1. Read Revelation 1:1. The word semaino (G4591) means "to signify, to communicate through signs." Nave's Topical Bible classifies the entire book of Revelation under VISION, and Rev 14:8-13 is specifically listed under VISION. What are the implications for reading Rev 14:10-11 as a literal didactic statement about human destiny?

    2. Rev 14:10 describes the torment occurring "in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb." The Greek enopion ("in the presence of / before") is a judgment/throne-room word throughout Revelation (4:5-6; 7:9; 8:2-4; 20:12). Does this suggest a permanent state or a judgment event?

    3. The "eis tous aionas ton aionon" ("for ever and ever" / "unto the ages of the ages") formula is used approximately 19 times for God and Christ in doxological contexts and 3 times in judgment contexts (Rev 14:11; 19:3; 20:10). When the same formula is applied to Babylon's smoke in Rev 19:3 -- a symbolic entity already described as utterly destroyed -- what does this tell you about whether the formula requires literal infinite duration in every context?

    Section E: Cross-Referencing Rev 14 with OT Judgment Imagery¶

    1. Read Psalm 37:20. "The wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away." What is smoke in this OT passage -- evidence of ongoing existence, or the end product of consumption?

    2. Read Hosea 13:3. Smoke is listed alongside morning cloud, early dew, and chaff as images of transience -- things that disappear. Now read Isaiah 51:6: "the heavens shall vanish away like smoke." What does the OT smoke vocabulary consistently represent -- permanence or vanishing?

    3. Read Ezekiel 28:18-19. Fire "shall devour thee" and produce "ashes upon the earth"; the subject is "never shalt thou be any more." What is the relationship between fire, ashes, and the final state described?

    4. Read Nahum 1:8-10. "He will make an utter end" and the enemies are "devoured as stubble fully dry." How does this fire-judgment passage describe the result -- as ongoing torment or as complete cessation?

    5. Read Psalm 11:6 alongside Revelation 14:10. Psalm 11:6 says "fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup." Rev 14:10 says "the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation." What OT imagery does Rev 14:10's "cup" language draw from, and what do OT judgment-cup passages describe -- an eternal state or a finite act of judgment?

    Section F: Rev 20:9-10 -- Different Subjects, Different Vocabulary¶

    1. Read Revelation 20:9-10 carefully. Verse 9 describes human enemies; verse 10 describes the devil, beast, and false prophet. Fill in the comparison:

      Rev 20:9 (humans) Rev 20:10 (devil, beast, false prophet)
      Subjects ___ ___
      Verb ___ ___
      Duration ___ ___
      Subject type Human ___

      The text uses different vocabulary for different subjects in adjacent verses. What does this distinction suggest?

    2. The beast and false prophet in Rev 20:10 are symbolic entities (the beast represents a political/religious system, not a literal animal). What are the implications of applying the fate of symbolic entities to literal human beings?
