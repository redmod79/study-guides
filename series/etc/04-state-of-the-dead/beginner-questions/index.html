    Study 4: State of the Dead — Conscious or Unconscious?¶

    What Does the Bible Say About the Condition of the Dead Between Death and Resurrection?

    A Note on How to Study

    In Study 1, we learned to distinguish between what the Bible explicitly says and what people infer from the Bible. These are not the same thing.

    • Explicit: You can point to a verse and quote it. The text directly says this.
    • Inference: Someone claims the Bible teaches something, but no single verse says it. They have drawn a conclusion — possibly a good one, possibly not — but the conclusion itself is not in the text.

    This distinction will be especially important in this study. Many popular beliefs about what happens to the dead come from inferences, not from explicit statements. We will look at every passage — including the ones people use to argue for consciousness after death — and ask: what does the text actually say?

    Section A: What Does the Old Testament Say About the Dead?¶

    1. Read Psalm 146:4. When a person dies and their breath departs, what happens to their thoughts? Does the text say this is gradual or immediate ("in that very day")?

    2. Read Ecclesiastes 9:5-6. What do the dead know? What has happened to their love, hatred, and envy?

    3. Read Ecclesiastes 9:10. What work, knowledge, or wisdom exists in the grave (sheol)?

    4. Read Psalm 6:5 and Psalm 115:17. Do the dead praise God? Is there remembrance of God in death? Where do the dead go?

    5. Read Job 14:10-12, 21. What happens to a man when he dies? Does he know what happens to his children?

    Section B: What Did Jesus and the Apostles Call Death?¶

    1. Read John 11:11-14. What did Jesus call Lazarus' condition? Then what did He clarify? Why is the word Jesus chose significant?

    2. Read Matthew 9:24 (also Mark 5:39 and Luke 8:52). What did Jesus say about the dead girl? How did the mourners react?

    3. Read Acts 7:60 and Acts 13:36. What language did Luke use for Stephen's death? What language did Paul use for David's death?

    4. Read 1 Corinthians 15:6, 18, 51 and 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14. What is Paul's standard term for the Christian dead? How many times does he use sleep language in these passages alone?

    Section C: What About Passages That Seem to Teach Consciousness After Death?¶

    1. Read 2 Corinthians 5:4, 8. Paul says he is willing to be "absent from the body and present with the Lord." But in verse 4, what does Paul say he wants — to be "unclothed" (disembodied) or "clothed upon" (given a new body)? Now read 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17. When does Paul say believers will "ever be with the Lord"?

    2. Read Luke 23:43. Jesus says "Verily I say unto thee, today shalt thou be with me in paradise" (KJV). The original Greek has no punctuation — the comma placement is an editorial decision. Now read John 20:17, where Jesus says on Sunday morning: "I am not yet ascended to my Father." Was Jesus in paradise on Friday?

    3. Read Luke 16:19-31. This passage follows a series of parables in Luke 15-16. What is the main teaching point stated in verses 29 and 31? Does any other Scripture describe "Abraham's bosom" as a real location for the dead?

    4. Read Revelation 6:9-11. This is an apocalyptic vision (the same vision with four horsemen, falling stars, and the sky rolling up). Now read Genesis 4:10 — Abel's blood "cries" from the ground. Does crying out require literal consciousness?

    Section D: When Do the Dead Receive Their Reward?¶

    1. Read 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17. What is the sequence Paul describes? When does "ever be with the Lord" begin — at death, or at the resurrection and Second Coming?

    2. Read Acts 2:29, 34. Peter says David is "both dead and buried." Has David ascended into the heavens?

    3. Read Hebrews 11:13, 39-40. Did the Old Testament heroes receive the promises when they died? What are they waiting for?

    4. Read 2 Timothy 4:8. When is the crown of righteousness given — at death, or at "that day" (Christ's appearing)?

    Explicit or Inference?¶

    For each statement below, decide: Does the Bible explicitly say this (you can point to a verse), or is it an inference (a conclusion someone draws that no verse directly states)?

    A. "The dead know not any thing." - [ ] Explicit — the Bible says this - [ ] Inference — someone concluded this

    B. "When a believer dies, they immediately go to heaven as a conscious being." - [ ] Explicit — the Bible says this - [ ] Inference — someone concluded this

    C. "David is not ascended into the heavens." - [ ] Explicit — the Bible says this - [ ] Inference — someone concluded this

    D. "The parable of the rich man and Lazarus describes the literal geography of the afterlife." - [ ] Explicit — the Bible says this - [ ] Inference — someone concluded this

    E. "The dead in Christ shall rise first: then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them... and so shall we ever be with the Lord." - [ ] Explicit — the Bible says this - [ ] Inference — someone concluded this
