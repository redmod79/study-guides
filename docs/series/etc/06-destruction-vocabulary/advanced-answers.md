# Study 6: Destruction Vocabulary -- Advanced Study Answer Key

*Hebrew and Greek Word Studies on the Fate of the Wicked*

---

> **Evidence Tiers**
>
> This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** -- The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** -- No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed -- every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
> 3. **Inference (I)** -- Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be *added* beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.
>
> The hierarchy matters: **Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference.** An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

## Section A: Hebrew Destruction Words -- Abad (H6) and Shamad (H8045)

**1. *Abad* (H6) in Psalm 37:20, 68:2, 73:27, 92:9, and Job 20:7.**

- Psalm 37:20: "The wicked shall perish (*abad*), and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away." Subject: the wicked.
- Psalm 68:2: "As wax melteth before the fire, so let the wicked perish (*abad*) at the presence of God." Subject: the wicked.
- Psalm 73:27: "They that are far from thee shall perish (*abad*)." Subject: those far from God.
- Psalm 92:9: "Thine enemies shall perish (*abad*); all the workers of iniquity shall be scattered." Subject: God's enemies.
- Job 20:7: "He shall perish (*abad*) for ever like his own dung: they which have seen him shall say, Where is he?" Subject: the wicked man. Note: "perish for ever" -- permanent destruction.

Collectively, these verses say the wicked will perish, and they use imagery of complete disappearance: consumed into smoke, melted like wax, vanished so that people ask "Where is he?" The word *abad* consistently describes cessation, not ongoing torment.

**2. *Shamad* (H8045) in Psalm 145:20, 37:38, 92:7, and Isaiah 13:9.**

- Psalm 145:20: "The LORD preserveth all them that love him: but all the wicked will he destroy (*shamad*)." The antithetical parallel is *preserve* vs. *destroy* -- continued existence vs. cessation.
- Psalm 37:38: "The transgressors shall be destroyed (*shamad*) together: the end of the wicked shall be cut off."
- Psalm 92:7: "They shall be destroyed (*shamad*) for ever." Permanent destruction.
- Isaiah 13:9: "He shall destroy (*shamad*) the sinners thereof out of it." The day of the LORD brings destruction.

*Shamad* means "to destroy utterly, to exterminate." In Psalm 145:20, the antithetical parallelism is critical: *preserve* and *shamad* are opposites. If *shamad* meant "ongoing existence in a ruined state," it would not be the opposite of "preserve."

**3. LXX translation of *abad* as *apollymi* 141 times.**

Pre-Christian Jewish translators working on the Septuagint chose *apollymi* to render *abad* 141 times -- the overwhelmingly dominant mapping. This establishes a verified lexical chain: when the NT authors used *apollymi* for the fate of the wicked (Matt 10:28; John 3:16; Luke 13:3; etc.), they were using the same word that the Greek-speaking Jewish world understood as the equivalent of *abad*. The semantic weight of *abad* (to perish, to be destroyed) carries directly into the NT through this translation tradition. This is not an inference -- it is an observable textual fact (#4a: SIS-verified cross-testament connection).

**4. Isaiah 26:14 -- both *shamad* and *abad* together.**

> "They are dead, they shall not live; they are deceased, they shall not rise: therefore hast thou visited and destroyed (*shamad*) them, and made all their memory to perish (*abad*)."

The verse uses both key destruction words and adds: (1) they shall not live, (2) they shall not rise, (3) their memory perishes. This is triple reinforcement of permanent cessation. Not only are they destroyed, but even the memory of them is obliterated. There is no ongoing existence described here in any form.

## Section B: Hebrew Destruction Words -- Kalah (H3615) and Shachath (H7843)

**5. *Kalah* (H3615) in Psalm 59:13, 104:35, and 73:19.**

- Psalm 59:13: "Consume (*kalah*) them in wrath, consume them, that they may not be (*ayin*)." The stated result of consumption is *ayin* -- non-existence. David does not pray for their torment; he prays for their consumption to the point of non-existence.
- Psalm 104:35: "Let the sinners be consumed (*kalah*) out of the earth, and let the wicked be no more (*ayin*)." Same result word: *ayin*.
- Psalm 73:19: "How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! they are utterly consumed (*kalah*) with terrors." Consumption is sudden and complete.

The LXX translates *kalah* as *synteleo* (to complete entirely, to consume) 85 times, *ekleipo* (to cease) 30 times, and *pauo* (to stop) 15 times. All three Greek renderings convey ending/cessation, not ongoing existence.

**6. *Shachath* (H7843) -- decay/corruption vs. torment.**

*Shachath* means "to corrupt, to decay, to ruin." The LXX renders it as *diaphtheiro* (to rot thoroughly, to destroy) 48 times and *phtheiro* (to corrupt, to destroy) 6 times. The semantic field is decay, decomposition, corruption -- the process by which something breaks down and ceases to maintain its form. This is the opposite of preservation. The "torment" semantic field (*basanizo*, *basanismos*) describes the experience of pain by a conscious subject. These are entirely different concepts: decay destroys the subject; torment presupposes the subject's continued existence. The destruction vocabulary consistently describes what happens to the subject itself, not what the subject experiences.

**7. Psalm 37:20 -- *abad* + *kalah* + smoke.**

> "The wicked shall perish (*abad*), and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume (*kalah*); into smoke shall they consume away."

Two destruction verbs reinforce each other: the wicked *perish* (abad) and *consume* (kalah). The smoke simile adds a visual: as sacrificial fat burns on the altar, it produces smoke that rises and disappears. The substance is consumed; the smoke dissipates. Nothing remains. The three elements (abad, kalah, smoke) all point in the same direction: the wicked cease to exist.

**8. Non-existence language: Psalm 59:13, 104:35, Obadiah 1:16.**

The Hebrew word *ayin* ("there is not" -- non-existence) appears in Psalm 59:13 ("that they may not be") and Psalm 104:35 ("let the wicked be no more"). Obadiah 1:16 uses *kelo hayu* ("as though they had not been") -- a different construction but the same concept. These are among the most explicit cessation statements in the OT. They do not describe ongoing existence in any form. They describe non-existence: the state of not being.

## Section C: Greek Destruction Words -- Apollymi (G622), Apoleia (G684), Olethros (G3639)

**9. *Apollymi* (G622) across six NT passages.**

- Matthew 10:28: God *destroys* (*apollymi*) soul and body in gehenna. Subject: human soul and body. Direct teaching of Jesus. Didactic genre.
- John 3:16: Whoever believes should not *perish* (*apollymi*) but have everlasting life. Subject: humanity. Contrast: perish vs. life.
- Luke 13:3: "Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise *perish* (*apollymi*)." Subject: Jesus' audience. Warning: repent or perish.
- Romans 2:12: "Sinned without law shall also *perish* (*apollymi*) without law." Subject: sinners. Paul's teaching.
- 1 Corinthians 15:18: If Christ is not raised, "they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are *perished* (*apollymi*)." Subject: dead believers. Paul's reductio ad absurdum -- without resurrection, the dead have perished.
- 2 Peter 3:9: "Not willing that any should *perish* (*apollymi*), but that all should come to repentance." Subject: all humanity. God's desire.

None of these contexts suggests "ongoing torment." Every usage places *apollymi* as the opposite of life, salvation, or resurrection. Strong's defines it as "to destroy fully." No lexicon defines it as "torment."

**10. *Apollymi* for wineskins -- Matthew 9:17.**

Burst wineskins *perish* (*apollymi*). They are not tormented. They burst, lose their form, and cease to function as wineskins. This non-theological usage reveals the word's basic meaning without any eschatological overlay: *apollymi* describes something that is destroyed and ceases to exist in its prior state. When Jesus uses this same word for the destruction of soul and body in gehenna (Matt 10:28), the word carries this same meaning.

**11. *Apoleia* (G684) across four NT passages.**

- Matthew 7:13: The broad way leads to *destruction* (*apoleia*). The destination is destruction, not torment.
- Philippians 3:19: "Whose end is *destruction* (*apoleia*)." The final outcome (*telos*) is destruction.
- 2 Peter 3:7: "Reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and *perdition* (*apoleia*) of ungodly men." Fire + destruction.
- Romans 9:22: "Vessels of wrath fitted to *destruction* (*apoleia*)." The vessels are destined for destruction -- not for ongoing existence in a different state.

Strong's defines *apoleia* as "ruin or loss." It is the noun form of *apollymi*. No passage defines *apoleia* as torment. In every case, it describes a terminal outcome.

**12. *Olethros* (G3639) -- "ruin, i.e. death, punishment."**

- 2 Thessalonians 1:9: "Punished with everlasting *destruction* (*olethros*) from the presence of the Lord." *Olethros* means "ruin, death." Paired with *aionios* (everlasting), it describes a destruction whose result is permanent -- not an ongoing process of being destroyed. Compare: "everlasting salvation" (Heb 5:9) is not an ongoing process of being saved. "Everlasting redemption" (Heb 9:12) is not an ongoing process of being redeemed. The pattern is: *aionios* + noun = a result that is permanent.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:3: "Sudden *destruction* (*olethros*) cometh upon them, and they shall not escape." Sudden and inescapable.
- 1 Timothy 6:9: "Drown men in *destruction* (*olethros*) and perdition (*apoleia*)." Two destruction words in parallel.

The OT parallels confirm the result-oriented reading: "destroyed (*shamad*) for ever" (Psa 92:7) and "perish (*abad*) for ever" (Job 20:7). Jude 1:7 clinches it: Sodom suffered "the vengeance of eternal fire," yet the cities are not still burning. "Eternal fire" produced a completed destruction whose result is permanent.

**13. *Olethros* + *apoleia* together in 1 Timothy 6:9.**

Paul pairs two destruction words from the same semantic field: *olethros* (ruin/death) and *apoleia* (destruction/loss). This double emphasis confirms he is working entirely within the destruction vocabulary, not the torment vocabulary. If Paul meant to describe ongoing torment, the Greek language had perfectly adequate words for that (*basanizo*, *basanismos*, *kolasis*) -- he did not use them. He used two destruction words instead.

## Section D: The "Perish vs. Life" Contrast Pattern

**14. The perish-vs-life contrast table.**

| Passage | Author | Outcome A (wicked) | Outcome B (righteous) |
|---|---|---|---|
| John 3:16 | John | Perish (*apollymi*) | Everlasting life (*zoe aionios*) |
| Matthew 7:13-14 | Jesus/Matthew | Destruction (*apoleia*) | Life (*zoe*) |
| Romans 6:23 | Paul | Death (*thanatos*) | Eternal life (*zoe aionios*) |
| Philippians 3:19 vs. 3:21 | Paul | Destruction (*apoleia*) | Glorified body |
| Psalm 145:20 | David | Destroy (*shamad*) | Preserve (*shamar*) |
| Galatians 6:8 | Paul | Corruption (*phthora*) | Life everlasting (*zoe aionios*) |

The pattern is consistent across at least four authors (John, Matthew/Jesus, Paul, David). In every case, the two outcomes are antithetical: life vs. death/destruction/perishing. The contrast only works if the two outcomes are genuinely opposite. "Everlasting life vs. everlasting conscious torment" would be two forms of continued existence -- not opposites. "Everlasting life vs. perishing" (cessation) presents genuine opposites. This structural pattern is a Necessary Implication (N028): both sides acknowledge the textual contrast exists; they disagree about what "perish" means.

**15. 1 Corinthians 15:18 -- Paul's reductio ad absurdum.**

Paul argues: if there is no resurrection, then the dead in Christ "are perished (*apollymi*)." His argument depends on *apollymi* meaning cessation -- they are gone, lost forever, no longer existing. If "perished" meant "still existing but in a spiritually ruined state," Paul's argument would collapse: the dead in Christ would still exist (just in a bad state), and the absence of resurrection would not be the catastrophe Paul presents it as. The horror of Paul's reductio is that without resurrection, death is final -- the dead have *ceased to exist*. This is one of the most revealing uses of *apollymi* in the NT because the word is applied to *believers*, not the wicked, showing what the word itself means apart from eschatological context.

## Section E: Destruction Similes

**16. Destruction similes catalog.**

| Passage | Simile | What happens to the substance? |
|---|---|---|
| Psalm 68:2 | Wax before fire | Melts, loses form, ceases to be wax |
| Psalm 37:20 | Fat of lambs / smoke | Fat burns away on altar; smoke rises and dissipates |
| Malachi 4:1 | Stubble in an oven | Burns up completely; "neither root nor branch" remains |
| Malachi 4:3 | Ashes | Inert residue after complete combustion; walked upon |
| Hosea 13:3 | Morning cloud, dew, chaff, smoke | Cloud dissipates, dew evaporates, chaff blows away, smoke disperses |
| Nahum 1:10 | Stubble fully dry | Consumed by fire instantly |
| Isaiah 1:31 | Tow (flax fiber) | Burns rapidly and completely |

Not a single simile depicts a substance that endures indefinitely. Every simile depicts something that ceases to exist through a natural process: melting, burning, evaporating, dissipating, blowing away. This is a Necessary Implication (N029): both sides accept what these substances naturally do. They disagree about whether the similes are meant to depict the actual fate or merely its severity.

**17. Isaiah 34:10 and Revelation 14:10-11 -- "smoke ascending forever."**

Isaiah 34:10 says of Edom: "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever." Edom is not still burning. The smoke is not still ascending. The land exists today (modern Jordan). "Smoke ascending forever" is prophetic/apocalyptic imagery for complete, irreversible destruction -- not a literal description of ongoing combustion. This OT precedent (#4a: SIS) governs the reading of Revelation 14:10-11, which uses the same imagery. If "smoke ascending forever" does not mean ongoing burning for Edom, it need not mean ongoing burning for the wicked.

**18. Jude 1:7 -- Sodom's "eternal fire."**

> "Even as Sodom and Gomorrha... are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire."

Sodom and Gomorrah are not still burning. The cities were destroyed -- completely, permanently, irreversibly. "Eternal fire" describes a fire whose *result* is eternal, not a fire whose *process* is eternal. This is the biblical example (#4a: SIS) that governs the reading of "everlasting destruction" in 2 Thessalonians 1:9: the destruction is completed; its effect is permanent.

## Section F: Destruction vs. Torment Vocabulary

**19. Destruction vs. torment vocabulary -- frequency and ratio.**

The destruction vocabulary (*apollymi*, *apoleia*, *olethros*, plus OT equivalents *abad*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *shachath*) accounts for approximately 743 combined OT/NT occurrences. The torment vocabulary (*basanizo*, *basanismos*, *kolasis*) is used for the fate of the wicked in a handful of passages, primarily Revelation 14:10-11 (apocalyptic genre), Revelation 20:10 (apocalyptic genre), and Matthew 25:46 (*kolasis*, which means "punishment," not necessarily torment). The ratio is approximately 30:1 in favor of destruction vocabulary. The Bible's dominant vocabulary for the fate of the wicked is destruction, not torment.

These are distinct word families with different semantic ranges (N030). No lexicon defines any destruction word as "torment," and no lexicon defines any torment word as "destroy." Using passages that employ torment vocabulary (Rev 14:10-11, in apocalyptic genre) to redefine the meaning of passages that employ destruction vocabulary (Matt 10:28, John 3:16, in didactic genre) requires an unverified cross-reference (#4b) between different word families.

**20. Revelation 14:10-11 vs. Matthew 10:28 -- vocabulary and genre.**

Revelation 14:10-11 uses *basanizo* (torment) -- torment vocabulary, not destruction vocabulary. It is apocalyptic genre (symbolic vision). Its subjects are specifically "beast-worshippers" within a highly symbolic narrative.

Matthew 10:28 uses *apollymi* (destroy) -- destruction vocabulary. It is didactic genre (direct teaching of Jesus). It addresses all hearers about the general fate of the wicked.

The more common vocabulary is destruction. The clearer genre is didactic. In the clarity hierarchy, didactic teaching governs apocalyptic imagery, not the other way around. Using the apocalyptic torment passage to redefine the didactic destruction passage inverts the interpretive hierarchy.

**Counter-argument -- apollymi in Luke 15 ("lost but still existing"):** The ECT position argues that *apollymi* can mean "lost but still existing" based on the lost sheep, lost coin, and lost son in Luke 15. This commits the illegitimate totality transfer -- importing one sense of a word into all of its occurrences. The active-voice eschatological uses (Matt 10:28: "destroy soul and body"; Matt 2:13: Herod seeking to "destroy" the child Jesus) confirm genuine destruction meaning. The lost son parable itself equates "lost" with "dead" (Luke 15:24, 32: "this my son was *dead*... was *lost*"), reinforcing rather than undermining the destruction reading.

## Evidence Classification Exercise -- Answers

**1. "The wicked shall perish" (Psa 37:20; Psa 68:2)**
- **Explicit (E).** These verses directly state this using the Hebrew word *abad*.

**2. "The LXX translates *abad* as *apollymi* 141 times; the same destruction vocabulary spans both testaments."**
- **Explicit (E).** This is an observable textual fact about the Septuagint translation. The data is verifiable.

**3. "No lexicon defines *abad*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *apollymi*, *apoleia*, or *olethros* as 'torment' or 'ongoing conscious suffering.'"**
- **Explicit (E).** This is a verifiable lexical fact. One can check every major Hebrew and Greek lexicon (BDB, HALOT, BDAG, Thayer, Strong's) and confirm that none of these words is defined as "torment."

**4. "The Bible's destruction vocabulary for the wicked means cessation of existence."**
- **Inference (I) -- specifically I-A (Evidence-Extending).** This systematizes approximately 40 explicit statements from 15+ biblical authors into a comprehensive claim. Every component is found in the explicit evidence. It is an inference only because it draws a conclusion from the cumulative pattern -- no concept is added beyond what the individual E-items already say. Criteria: #5 (systematizing), #4a (LXX-verified vocabulary chain).

**5. "The destruction words are metaphorical: 'perish' means spiritual ruin (continued existence in a ruined state)."**
- **Inference (I) -- specifically I-B (Competing Evidence).** This claim requires every destruction word to mean something other than its lexical definition. No lexicon defines any of these words as "spiritual ruin" or "continued existence in a ruined state." The FOR evidence comes from torment-vocabulary passages (Rev 14:10-11, using *basanizo*, in apocalyptic genre) and one parabolic image (Matt 13:42, wailing in furnace). The AGAINST evidence comes from the lexical definitions themselves, from 40+ didactic passages using destruction vocabulary, and from explicit non-existence statements ("shall not be," "be no more," "as though they had not been"). Criteria: #1 (adding a meaning not in lexicons), #2 (choosing between lexical and metaphorical readings).

**6. "'Everlasting destruction' means an ongoing process of being destroyed."**
- **Inference (I) -- specifically I-B (Competing Evidence).** *Olethros* means "ruin, death" -- a result, not a process. The claim requires "everlasting" to modify the process rather than the result. Compare: "everlasting salvation" (Heb 5:9) is not an ongoing process of being saved. "Everlasting redemption" (Heb 9:12) is not an ongoing process of being redeemed. Jude 1:7's "eternal fire" applied to Sodom demonstrates the result-oriented reading: the destruction was completed; its effect is permanent. The OT parallels "destroyed for ever" (Psa 92:7) and "perish for ever" (Job 20:7) confirm the same pattern. Criteria: #2 (choosing between result-oriented and process-oriented readings).

**7. "'Wailing and gnashing of teeth' in a furnace describes eternal conscious torment."**
- **Inference (I) -- specifically I-B (Competing Evidence).** The furnace passage is parabolic (Matt 13:42, the parable of the tares). The same author, Matthew, uses destruction vocabulary in didactic teaching about the same topic: "destroy soul and body in gehenna" (Matt 10:28) and "the way that leads to destruction" (Matt 7:13). Didactic teaching governs parabolic imagery in the clarity hierarchy. "Wailing and gnashing" could describe the moment of being cast in (anguish at the point of destruction), not an ongoing state. The text does not specify duration. Criteria: #2 (choosing between momentary anguish and ongoing state), #3 (parabolic genre treated as didactic).

## What CAN Be Said / What CANNOT Be Said -- Answers

**What CAN be said (Scripture explicitly states or necessarily implies):**

1. The Bible's primary vocabulary for the fate of the wicked consists of destruction/cessation words. Four Hebrew words (*abad*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *shachath*) totaling approximately 627 occurrences and three Greek words (*apollymi*, *apoleia*, *olethros*) totaling approximately 116 occurrences form the destruction vocabulary. No lexicon defines any of these words as "torment."

2. The LXX establishes verified cross-testament vocabulary continuity: *abad* maps to *apollymi* 141 times, *shamad* to *apollymi* 23 times, *shachath* to *diaphtheiro* 48 times, *kalah* to *synteleo* 85 times. When the NT uses *apollymi*, it uses the same word the LXX uses for *abad*.

3. The "perish vs. life" contrast appears across multiple authors (John 3:16; Matt 7:13-14; Rom 6:23; Phil 3:19; Psa 145:20; Gal 6:8). Life and destruction are presented as opposite outcomes.

4. All biblical destruction similes depict substances that cease to exist: wax, smoke, chaff, stubble, ashes, dew, morning clouds, fat, tow. Not one simile depicts a substance that endures indefinitely.

5. Multiple passages state the result of destruction as non-existence: "the wicked shall not be" (Psa 37:10), "that they may not be" (Psa 59:13), "be no more" (Psa 104:35), "as though they had not been" (Oba 1:16).

6. "Everlasting destruction" (*olethros aionios*, 2 Thess 1:9) combines "everlasting" with a word meaning "ruin, death." Jude 1:7 demonstrates that "eternal fire" applied to Sodom describes a completed destruction whose result is permanent -- not an ongoing process.

**What CANNOT be said (not stated or necessarily implied -- requires adding something):**

1. **It cannot be said that destruction vocabulary means "ongoing conscious torment."** No lexicon defines *abad*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *shachath*, *apollymi*, *apoleia*, or *olethros* as "torment" or "ongoing suffering." Reading these words as "spiritual ruin" requires adding a meaning the text does not contain.

2. **It cannot be said that the destruction similes depict ongoing existence.** Chaff, wax, smoke, stubble, ashes, morning clouds, and dew all cease to exist through natural processes. Reading them as depicting something that endures requires them to mean the opposite of what the natural processes illustrate.

3. **It cannot be said that "everlasting destruction" means an ongoing process of being destroyed.** *Olethros* means "ruin/death" (a result, not a process). The biblical example of "eternal fire" applied to Sodom (Jude 1:7) demonstrates the result-oriented reading.

4. **It cannot be said that torment vocabulary defines what destruction vocabulary means.** *Basanizo* (torment) and *apollymi* (destroy) are different words from different word families. Using Revelation 14:10-11 (*basanizo*, apocalyptic genre) to define what *apollymi* means in Matthew 10:28 (didactic genre) requires an unverified cross-reference between different word families.

5. **It cannot be said that "wailing and gnashing of teeth" in a furnace proves eternal conscious torment.** The furnace passages are parabolic (Matt 13:42, 50). The same author uses destruction vocabulary in didactic teaching (Matt 10:28; 7:13). Didactic governs parabolic.

6. **It cannot be said that the destruction vocabulary is insignificant or merely metaphorical.** Approximately 743 occurrences across both testaments from virtually every biblical author constitute the Bible's dominant vocabulary for the fate of the wicked. This is not a marginal vocabulary set that can be overridden by a few passages from a single genre.
