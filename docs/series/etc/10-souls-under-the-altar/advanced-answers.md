# Study 10: Souls Under the Altar -- Advanced Study Answer Key

*Revelation 6:9-11 -- Apocalyptic Vision, Sacrificial Imagery, and the State of the Dead*

---

> **Evidence Tiers**
>
> This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** -- The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** -- No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed -- every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
> 3. **Inference (I)** -- Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be *added* beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.
>
> The hierarchy matters: **Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference.** An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

## Section A: Word Studies -- *Sphazō*, *Psychē*, *Anapauō*

**1. *Sphazō* (G4969, "to slaughter/slay") occurs only 8 times in the entire NT: Rev 5:6,9,12 (the Lamb); Rev 6:4 (general killing); Rev 6:9 (the martyrs); Rev 13:3 (the beast, parodying the Lamb); Rev 18:24 (all slain on earth); 1 Jn 3:12 (Abel). The word is a sacrificial/butchering term. Chart all 8 uses. What framework emerges from the concentrated use of *sphazō* for the Lamb, the martyrs, and Abel?**

| # | Reference | Subject | Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rev 5:6 | The Lamb | "A Lamb as it had been slain" |
| 2 | Rev 5:9 | The Lamb | "Thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us" |
| 3 | Rev 5:12 | The Lamb | "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain" |
| 4 | Rev 6:4 | General | "That they should kill [*sphazō*] one another" |
| 5 | Rev 6:9 | The martyrs | "Souls of them that were slain" |
| 6 | Rev 13:3 | The beast | "As it were wounded [*sphazō*] to death" (parodying the Lamb) |
| 7 | Rev 18:24 | All slain | "All that were slain upon the earth" |
| 8 | 1 Jn 3:12 | Abel | Cain "slew his brother" |

The framework is sacrificial. *Sphazō* is a butchering/slaughtering term used in the LXX for sacrificial killing. The concentrated use in Revelation creates a vocabulary chain: Lamb -- martyrs -- Abel. The martyrs are presented as sacrifice-victims whose blood-life has been offered. Their placement "under the altar" corresponds to where sacrificial blood collects. Rev 13:3 shows the beast parodying the Lamb's *sphazō* -- confirming the sacrificial framework as deliberate.

**2. *Psychē* (G5590) is used multiple ways in Revelation.**

| Reference | Translation | Subject | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rev 6:9 | "souls" | slain martyrs | blood-lives of sacrifice-victims |
| Rev 8:9 | "life" | sea creatures | animal life |
| Rev 12:11 | "lives" | the same martyrs | physical lives |
| Rev 16:3 | "living soul" | sea creatures | animal life |
| Rev 20:4 | "souls" | beheaded martyrs | same group as Rev 6:9 |

*Psychē* in Revelation does not exclusively mean "disembodied conscious spirit." It is used for animal life (Rev 8:9; 16:3), for the physical lives of martyrs ("loved not their lives unto the death," Rev 12:11), and for the blood-lives of the slain at the altar base (Rev 6:9; 20:4). The word carries the same range as Hebrew *nephesh*: living being, life, blood-life. Reading *psychē* as "disembodied conscious spirit" in Rev 6:9 requires importing a meaning the word does not carry elsewhere in the same book.

**3. The OT establishes that *nephesh* is in the blood. If *psychē* = *nephesh* = blood = life, what does "souls [*psychas*] under the altar" correspond to in OT sacrificial practice?**

It corresponds to blood poured at the base of the altar. The logical chain: *psychē* = *nephesh* (LXX equivalence); *nephesh* = blood (Lev 17:11,14; Deut 12:23; Gen 9:4); sacrificial blood goes to the bottom of the altar (Lev 4:7,18,25,30,34; Exo 29:12). Therefore *psychas* "under" (*hypokatō*) the altar = blood-life at the altar base. The spatial correspondence between the OT practice and John's vision is exact.

**4. *Anapauō* (G373, "to rest") comparison:**

| Passage | Command/Description | Subject | What follows the rest? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rev 6:11 | "They should rest yet for a little season" | Martyrs under the altar | Fellow servants complete their course |
| Rev 14:13 | "They may rest from their labours" | The blessed dead | Their works follow them |
| Dan 12:13 | "Thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days" | Daniel | Resurrection at the end |

All three describe rest as a condition that precedes a future event -- not as conscious activity. The *anapauō* in Rev 6:11 and 14:13 is the same word by the same author in the same book. Daniel's *nuach* parallels the pattern: rest, then stand (rise). None of the three passages identifies "rest" as conscious enjoyment; all place resurrection or future vindication as the event that follows the rest.

## Section B: Genre and Subject Gate Analysis (Tree 3)

**5. Rev 1:1 uses *sēmainō* to describe how the book communicates. Nave's classifies Revelation under VISION. Gate 3 answer?**

Gate 3 FAIL. The genre is apocalyptic vision. Rev 1:1 states the book communicates through signs (*sēmainō*). Nave's classifies the entire book under VISION. The seal sequence contains colored horses, personified Death and Hades, cosmic upheaval where stars fall and heaven rolls up. This is apocalyptic vision literature, not didactic teaching about the state of the dead.

**6. The fourth seal (Rev 6:8) personifies Death and Hades as horsemen. Gate 1 assessment?**

Gate 1 FAIL. The subjects are symbolic vision-figures. The immediately preceding seal personifies abstract concepts (Death, Hades) as literal riders. The *psychas* under the altar are vision-figures in the same symbolic sequence -- blood-lives at the altar base, presented as sacrifice-victims within the Lamb-martyrs-Abel framework. They are not literal human beings observed in their actual post-mortem condition.

**7. In Rev 16:7, the altar itself speaks. Does the speech of the souls require literal consciousness?**

No. If the altar can speak in this book ("I heard another out of the altar say, Even so, Lord God Almighty, true and righteous are thy judgments," Rev 16:7), then the speech of the souls under the altar fits the same personification pattern. Personification of non-speaking entities is established within Revelation itself. The speech functions as a narrative device -- the shed blood demands vindication, following the Gen 4:10 pattern.

**8. Tree 3 application to Rev 6:9-11:**

- **Step 1 (Vocabulary Scan):** "Cried with a loud voice" (Rev 6:10) registers as V2 (conscious activity vocabulary). Candidate: ECT. Proceed to Step 2.

- **Step 2, Gate 1 (Subject Gate):** The *psychas* under the altar are vision-figures in an apocalyptic vision. The preceding seal personifies Death and Hades as horsemen. All surrounding seals use symbolic imagery. **FAIL: Subject is a symbolic vision-figure.**

- **Step 2, Gate 3 (Genre Gate):** Revelation is apocalyptic vision literature (*sēmainō*, Rev 1:1; Nave's VISION classification). The seal sequence contains colored horses, personified Death/Hades, cosmic upheaval. **FAIL: Genre is apocalyptic vision.**

- **Step 3 (Reclassification):** Gate 1 failure and Gate 3 failure. Corrected observation: "In an apocalyptic vision, John sees symbolic figures (blood-life under the heavenly altar) who cry out for vindication and are told to rest. The blood-of-Abel parallel (Gen 4:10) provides the interpretive precedent: shed blood cries to God without the dead person being conscious." Symbolic apocalyptic imagery of blood-life crying for vindication does not constitute didactic teaching about the literal state of the dead.

- **Result: Neutral.** Both sides accept the textual observation. The interpretation of whether this depicts literal consciousness is disputed.

## Section C: The OT Sacrificial Altar Background

**9. Document the blood-at-the-altar-base legislation.**

Six passages specify blood poured at the bottom of the altar:

| Passage | Text |
|---|---|
| Lev 4:7 | "shall pour all the blood of the bullock at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering" |
| Lev 4:18 | "shall pour out all the blood at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering" |
| Lev 4:25 | "shall pour out his blood at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering" |
| Lev 4:30 | "shall pour out all the blood thereof at the bottom of the altar" |
| Lev 4:34 | "shall pour out all the blood thereof at the bottom of the altar" |
| Exo 29:12 | "pour all the blood beside the bottom of the altar" |

In every case, the spatial relationship is the same: blood is at the base -- under -- the altar. This is the exact position where John sees the *psychas* in Rev 6:9.

**10. The Greek *hypokatō* ("under, beneath") places the *psychas* beneath the altar. Is the correspondence incidental?**

The correspondence is not incidental. The OT sacrificial background explains the imagery: *psychas* (blood-lives) are located *hypokatō* (under/beneath) the altar -- precisely where sacrificial blood was poured in every Levitical sacrifice. John, steeped in OT imagery, sees the blood-lives of sacrifice-victims at the altar base. The *sphazō* vocabulary confirms the sacrificial framework. The entire vision is built on OT altar theology.

**11. Read Revelation 18:24. "In her was found the blood of prophets, and of saints, and of all that were slain [*sphazō*] upon the earth." Conscious souls or blood-guilt?**

Blood-guilt. No one reads Rev 18:24 as saying conscious disembodied souls are found "inside" Babylon. The blood of the slain found in Babylon = blood-guilt. Babylon is held accountable for shedding their blood. The same *sphazō* used for the martyrs in Rev 6:9 appears here. The pattern is consistent: the language describes blood-guilt and the demand for vindication, not the literal location of conscious spirits.

## Section D: The Abel-to-Martyrs Arc

**12. Trace the Abel-blood-crying motif across Scripture.**

The arc traces as follows:

1. **Origin (Gen 4:10):** Abel's blood cries from the ground -- forensic personification.
2. **Confirmation (Heb 11:4):** Abel, dead, yet speaks -- through sacrifice, not consciousness.
3. **Extension (Heb 12:24):** Christ's blood speaks better things than Abel's -- blood speaks.
4. **Jesus' connection (Matt 23:35; Luke 11:50-51):** All righteous blood from Abel through the prophets is connected as a single stream of blood-guilt.
5. **Vocabulary link (1 Jn 3:12):** Cain *sphazō*-ed Abel -- same sacrificial term used for the Lamb and martyrs.
6. **Revelation climax (Rev 6:9-10):** The *psychas* of the *sphazō*-ed cry for vindication -- same motif, same vocabulary.
7. **Babylon's guilt (Rev 17:6; 18:24):** Babylon drunk with blood; the blood of all *sphazō*-ed found in her.
8. **Resolution (Rev 19:2):** God "hath avenged [*ekdikeō*] the blood of his servants" -- the cry is answered.

The narrative arc runs from Abel's blood crying in Genesis to God avenging His servants' blood in Revelation 19. The cry of Rev 6:10 is the same *ekdikeō* ("avenge") that is resolved in Rev 19:2 -- the *ekdikeō* bookend. This functions as a prophetic-narrative device driving the judgment sequence, not as a description of the intermediate state. The cry opens a vindication arc; the vindication at Rev 19:2 closes it.

**13. Does Jesus treat Abel's crying blood as evidence of consciousness or as a forensic demand for justice?**

Jesus treats it as a forensic demand for justice against the guilty. "That upon you may come all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of righteous Abel unto the blood of Zacharias" (Matt 23:35). The subject is accountability -- the persecutors will answer for the blood. Jesus does not reference Abel's consciousness; he references Abel's blood as the beginning of a stream of blood-guilt that demands justice. Luke 11:50-51 makes the same point: "the blood of all the prophets... may be required of this generation."

**14. Does the sequence between Rev 6:10 and Rev 19:2 function as a description of the intermediate state or a literary arc of vindication?**

A literary arc of vindication. The sequence: blood cries for justice (6:10) -- blood-for-blood judgment (16:6) -- altar affirms (16:7) -- Babylon drunk with blood (17:6) -- blood of all slain found in Babylon (18:24) -- God avenges (19:2). Every element concerns the shedding and avenging of blood. The *ekdikeō* bookend (6:10; 19:2) frames the arc. The cry opens the question ("How long?"); the vindication answers it. Nowhere in this sequence is the conscious intermediate state of the dead the subject. The subject is justice for shed blood.

## Section E: The Rev 20:4-5 Connection and the Rest-Then-Resurrection Pattern

**15. Compare the vocabulary of Rev 6:9 and Rev 20:4.**

| Element | Rev 6:9 | Rev 20:4 |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects | *psychas* (souls) | *psychas* (souls) |
| Death | *esphagmenōn* (slain/slaughtered) | *pepelekismenōn* (beheaded) |
| Cause 1 | *dia ton logon tou Theou* (for the word of God) | *dia ton logon tou Theou* (for the word of God) |
| Cause 2 | *dia tēn martyrian* (for their testimony) | *dia tēn martyrian Iēsou* (for the testimony of Jesus) |

Three vocabulary elements match exactly: *psychas*, *logos tou Theou*, *martyria*. The death is described with different specific words (*sphazō* vs. *pelekizō*) but both denote violent execution. Same author, same book, same subjects, same causes. This is the same group.

**16. If the martyrs' transition to "living" is identified as the first resurrection, what was their condition before?**

They were not living. Rev 20:4-5: "They lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. This is the first resurrection." The text explicitly identifies their "living" as the first resurrection -- that is the moment they begin to live. Before the first resurrection, they were among those who "lived not" (Rev 20:5a). Their condition prior to the first resurrection was death, not conscious intermediate existence.

**17. Does "lived not" match or contradict the claim that they are consciously alive under the altar?**

It contradicts it. If the martyrs of Rev 6:9 are the same group as Rev 20:4 (confirmed by vocabulary overlap), and Rev 20:4-5 states that their "living" is the first resurrection, then before the first resurrection they "lived not." The claim that they are consciously alive under the altar between death and resurrection contradicts the statement that they "lived" at the first resurrection. You cannot begin to "live" at a resurrection if you were already living.

**18. Document the three-passage rest-then-resurrection pattern.**

| Passage | Rest Command | Subject | What Follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rev 6:11 | *anapauō* -- "rest yet for a little season" | Martyrs under the altar | Fellow servants complete their testimony |
| Rev 14:13 | *anapauō* -- "rest from their labours" | The blessed dead | "Their works do follow them" |
| Dan 12:13 | *nuach* -- "thou shalt rest" | Daniel | "Stand in thy lot at the end of the days" |

All three passages describe rest as a temporary condition that precedes a future event (completion of the martyrs' company, the following of works, resurrection at the end). None describes rest as conscious activity or enjoyment. Daniel's passage is the most explicit: rest, THEN stand (rise). The pattern across all three is the same: death = rest, awaiting resurrection.

## Section F: Counter-Arguments and the SIS Hierarchy

**19. Does "crying" in apocalyptic vision literature prove literal consciousness?**

No. The pattern of non-personal entities "crying" is established across both testaments: Abel's blood cries (Gen 4:10); wages cry (Jas 5:4); stones cry out (Luke 19:40; Hab 2:11). Within Revelation itself, the altar speaks (Rev 16:7), Death and Hades ride horses (Rev 6:8), and the book communicates through signs (Rev 1:1). The claim that the souls' crying proves consciousness requires treating one instance of apocalyptic personification as literal while accepting all others in the same context as symbolic. No textual marker distinguishes the souls' speech from the altar's speech or Death's horseback ride.

**20. Do symbolic entities receiving symbolic items constitute evidence of literal conscious activity?**

No. In Revelation, the Lamb has seven horns and seven eyes (Rev 5:6) -- no one claims a literal lamb has literal extra horns and eyes. Babylon is a woman dressed in purple and scarlet (Rev 17:4) -- no one claims a literal woman is the city. The dragon has seven heads (Rev 12:3) -- no one claims a literal seven-headed dragon exists. White robes are symbolic in Revelation (Rev 3:4-5 for overcomers; Rev 7:9,13-14 for those who washed robes in the Lamb's blood). Symbolic entities receiving symbolic items in an apocalyptic vision does not constitute evidence of literal conscious reward. The white robes represent vindication -- the martyrs' cause is affirmed.

**21. Do all symbols refer to the literal version of their imagery?**

No. Symbols refer to realities, but not to the literal version of the symbol. The symbol of Death riding a horse refers to the reality of widespread death -- not to Death literally riding a horse. The symbol of a woman on seven hills refers to a persecuting power -- not to a literal woman sitting on literal hills. The symbol of a seven-eyed Lamb refers to Christ in his omniscience and sacrificial nature -- not to a literal seven-eyed lamb. Similarly, the symbol of souls under the altar refers to the reality that God remembers the blood of his martyrs and will vindicate them -- not to literal conscious disembodied souls stationed at a literal altar in heaven. The reality behind the symbol is the demand for justice, not the literal post-mortem condition depicted.

**22. Can an apocalyptic vision override multiple didactic statements from multiple authors?**

No. The SIS clarity criteria rank didactic above apocalyptic on all five measures:

| Criterion | Didactic death-state passages | Rev 6:9-11 |
|---|---|---|
| Directness | Direct statement ("the dead know not any thing") | Symbolic vision within a seal sequence |
| Genre | Wisdom literature, psalms, prophetic prose | Apocalyptic vision |
| Scope | Universal statements about all the dead | One vision of one group |
| Frequency | 8+ authors, 10+ passages | One passage |
| Self-interpretation | No genre markers requiring decoding | *Sēmainō* (Rev 1:1) -- book communicates by signs |

Eight or more OT authors across six or more books describe the dead as unconscious (Ecc 9:5; Ps 146:4; 115:17; 6:5; Ecc 9:10; Job 14:12,21; Isa 38:18-19). These are direct, didactic statements. Rev 6:9-11 is a symbolic vision within a book that declares it communicates through signs. The didactic governs the apocalyptic, not the reverse. An inference drawn from apocalyptic vision imagery cannot override what multiple authors explicitly state in didactic literature.

## Section G: Evidence Classification Exercise -- Answers

**1. "In an apocalyptic vision, John sees *psychas* under the altar -- those slain (*sphazō*) for the word of God and for their testimony."**
- **Classification: E (Explicit).** Rev 6:9 directly states this. The apocalyptic genre is confirmed by Rev 1:1 and the vision context.

**2. "*Psyche/nephesh* = blood = life (Lev 17:11,14; Gen 9:4; Deut 12:23)."**
- **Classification: E (Explicit).** Multiple passages directly state this equation: "the life [*nephesh*] of the flesh is in the blood" (Lev 17:11); "the blood is the life [*nephesh*]" (Deut 12:23).

**3. "The *psychas* under the altar correspond to sacrificial blood at the altar base, following the OT pattern."**
- **Classification: N (Necessary Implication).** If *psychē* = *nephesh* = blood (E), and sacrificial blood was poured at the altar base (E), then *psychas* under the altar = blood-life at the altar base. No added concept is required; the conclusion follows unavoidably from the two explicit premises.

**4. "Rev 6:9-11 teaches that the dead are consciously present in heaven, aware of earthly events, and communicating with God."**
- **Classification: I (Inference).** No verse states this conclusion. It requires treating apocalyptic vision imagery as a literal description of the intermediate state -- an external hermeneutical framework. The passage fails Gate 1 (symbolic vision-figure) and Gate 3 (apocalyptic genre) of Tree 3. The Abel parallel (Gen 4:10) demonstrates that crying does not require consciousness. The altar itself speaks (Rev 16:7). What is added: the assumption that apocalyptic personification equals literal description of consciousness.

**5. "The sphazō vocabulary chain links the Lamb, the martyrs, and Abel in a shared sacrificial framework."**
- **Classification: N (Necessary Implication).** *Sphazō* is used for all three (Rev 5:6,9,12; 6:9; 18:24; 1 Jn 3:12). The shared vocabulary places them in the same framework. Both sides accept this observation.

**6. "The white robes prove the dead receive rewards before the resurrection."**
- **Classification: I (Inference).** No verse states that the white robes prove conscious reception of pre-resurrection rewards. White robes are symbolic in Revelation (Rev 3:4-5; 7:9,13-14). The claim requires literalizing apocalyptic symbolism. What is added: the framework of treating symbolic actions in a vision as evidence of literal conscious activity.

**7. "The same group appears in Rev 20:4-5 and 'lived' at the first resurrection."**
- **Classification: E (Explicit).** The vocabulary overlap (*psychas*, *martyria*, *logos tou Theou*) identifies the same group. Rev 20:4-5 directly states: "they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. This is the first resurrection."

## Section H: What CAN Be Said / What CANNOT Be Said -- Answers

**What CAN be said (the Bible explicitly states or necessarily implies):**

1. Revelation 6:9-11 occurs within an apocalyptic vision sequence where all surrounding elements are symbolic (colored horses, personified Death and Hades, cosmic upheaval). Nave's classifies Revelation under VISION. Rev 1:1 states the book communicates through signs (*sēmainō*).

2. The *psychas* (souls/lives) under the altar correspond to the OT sacrificial blood poured at the base of the altar (Lev 4:7,18,25,30,34; Exo 29:12). *Psychē* = *nephesh* = blood = life (Lev 17:11,14; Gen 9:4; Deut 12:23). The souls are under (*hypokatō*) the altar -- where sacrificial blood collects.

3. The *sphazō* vocabulary chain links the Lamb (Rev 5:6,9,12), the martyrs (Rev 6:9; 18:24), and Abel (1 Jn 3:12) in a shared sacrificial framework. The martyrs are presented as sacrifice-victims whose blood-life is at the altar base.

4. Abel's blood cried from the ground (Gen 4:10) without Abel being conscious. Hebrews 11:4 states Abel "being dead yet speaketh" through sacrifice. Hebrews 12:24 states Christ's blood "speaketh." The pattern of blood speaking/crying is forensic personification.

5. Non-personal entities cry across both testaments: wages cry (Jas 5:4), stones cry out (Luke 19:40; Hab 2:11). The altar itself speaks in Rev 16:7.

6. The martyrs are told to "rest" (*anapauō*, Rev 6:11), the same word used in Rev 14:13 for the blessed dead who rest from labors. Daniel 12:13 uses the Hebrew equivalent: rest, then stand at the end.

7. The same group appears in Rev 20:4-5, identified by matching vocabulary (*psychas*, *martyria*, *logos tou Theou*). They "lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. This is the first resurrection." The transition to living occurs at the resurrection.

8. The cry of Rev 6:10 (*ekdikeō*, "avenge our blood") is answered in Rev 19:2 (*ekdikeō*, "hath avenged the blood of his servants"). The cry functions as a prophetic-narrative device, rooted in Deut 32:43.

9. At least eight OT authors across six or more books describe the dead as unconscious (Ecc 9:5; Ps 146:4; 115:17; 6:5; Ecc 9:10; Job 14:12,21; Isa 38:18-19). These are didactic passages. Rev 6:9-11 is apocalyptic vision. SIS clarity criteria: didactic > apocalyptic.

10. *Psychē* in Revelation is used for animal life (Rev 8:9; 16:3) and for "lives" (Rev 12:11), not exclusively for a disembodied conscious spirit. The word carries the full range of *nephesh*.

**What CANNOT be said (not stated or necessarily implied -- requires adding something):**

1. **It cannot be said that Rev 6:9-11 teaches the dead are conscious.** The passage is an apocalyptic vision (Gate 3 FAIL), the subjects are symbolic vision-figures (Gate 1 FAIL), and the blood-of-Abel parallel demonstrates that "crying" does not require consciousness. The altar itself speaks in Rev 16:7. Deriving a doctrine of conscious intermediate existence from apocalyptic imagery requires an external framework (I-C).

2. **It cannot be said that the "souls" are disembodied conscious entities.** *Psychē/nephesh* = blood = life (Lev 17:11,14). In the OT, sacrificial blood was poured at the altar base (Lev 4:7). The *psychas* under the altar correspond to blood-life at the altar base. *Psychē* is used for animal life (Rev 8:9; 16:3) and for physical "lives" (Rev 12:11) in the same book.

3. **It cannot be said that the white robes prove the dead receive rewards before resurrection.** White robes are symbolic in Revelation (Rev 3:4-5; 7:9,13-14). The Lamb has seven horns and seven eyes (Rev 5:6). Symbolic entities receive symbolic actions in apocalyptic visions.

4. **It cannot be said that this passage overrides the didactic death-state testimony.** Eight or more OT authors describe the dead as unconscious in didactic literature. Rev 6:9-11 is apocalyptic vision imagery. SIS clarity criteria place didactic above apocalyptic in every category. An apocalyptic vision cannot override didactic teaching.

5. **It cannot be said that the martyrs are "living" in an intermediate state.** Rev 20:4-5, using the same vocabulary, locates their "living" at the first resurrection. Before the first resurrection, they "lived not" (Rev 20:5a). The text places their transition to living at the resurrection, not in an intermediate state.
