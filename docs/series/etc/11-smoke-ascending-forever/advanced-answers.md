# Study 11: Smoke Ascending Forever -- Advanced Study Answer Key

*Revelation 14:9-11 and 19:3 -- OT Source Imagery, Greek Vocabulary, and Genre Analysis*

---

> **Evidence Tiers**
>
> This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** -- The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** -- No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed -- every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
> 3. **Inference (I)** -- Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be *added* beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.
>
> The hierarchy matters: **Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference.** An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

## Section A: The OT Fire-and-Brimstone Paradigm

**1. Fire-and-brimstone paradigm across the OT:**

| Passage | Subject | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 19:24-25 | Sodom and Gomorrah | "He overthrew those cities" -- destruction |
| Deuteronomy 29:23 | The land under God's curse | "Brimstone, and salt, and burning" -- desolation (compared to the overthrow of Sodom) |
| Psalm 11:6 | The wicked | "Fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup" -- judgment poured out |
| Isaiah 30:33 | Tophet (prepared for the king) | "The pile thereof is fire and much wood; the breath of the LORD, like a stream of brimstone, doth kindle it" -- a burning pyre that consumes |
| Isaiah 34:9-10 | Edom | Land becomes burning pitch; smoke goes up "for ever" -- but animals inhabit the ruins (34:11-17) |
| Ezekiel 38:22 | Gog's armies | "Fire, and brimstone" -- followed by seven months of burying corpses (39:11-12) |

In no verifiable case does the fire-and-brimstone paradigm produce ongoing conscious torment. Every instance describes an act of destructive judgment with a permanent result. Sodom was overthrown. Edom was desolated (animals inhabit it). Gog's armies are dead (corpses are buried). The paradigm means destruction.

**2. All 7 OT occurrences of *gophriyth* (H1614, brimstone):**

All seven occurrences describe brimstone as an agent of destructive judgment. Zero occurrences describe ongoing conscious torment. The distribution is 7-for-destruction, 0-for-torment. This is observable data, not interpretation.

**3. Luke 17:29 -- Jesus on fire and brimstone:**

> "But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all." -- Luke 17:29

This is the only non-Revelation NT use of *theion* (G2303, brimstone). Jesus' own description of the fire-and-brimstone paradigm uses the verb "destroyed" (*apollymi*) -- the standard NT destruction word. Jesus says the fire and brimstone destroyed them all. Not "tormented them all." Not "confined them in conscious suffering." Destroyed.

**4. Ezekiel 38:22 and 39:11-12:**

Ezek 38:22 describes fire-and-brimstone judgment on Gog. Ezek 39:11-12 describes the aftermath: "And seven months shall the house of Israel be burying of them." The fire-and-brimstone judgment produces corpses requiring seven months of burial. The dead are not being tormented -- they are being buried. This completes the OT brimstone chain: every instance from Gen 19 through Ezek 38 results in death and destruction.

## Section B: Isaiah 34 -- The Source Text for "Smoke Ascending Forever"

**5. Isaiah 34:9-17 -- the strongest "forever" language and its outcome:**

Isaiah 34:10 uses three durational expressions for Edom's judgment: (1) "not quenched night nor day," (2) "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever," and (3) "from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever" (using *le-netsach netsachim*, the strongest available "forever" form in Hebrew). Despite this -- the strongest "forever" language the OT can produce -- Isaiah 34:11-17 describes cormorants, bitterns, owls, ravens, jackals, wild goats, and vultures permanently dwelling in the territory.

This demonstrates that the "forever fire/smoke" formula describes the permanence of the *result* (irreversible desolation), not the perpetuity of the *process* (ongoing burning). If the fire were still burning and the smoke still physically ascending, animals could not dwell there and owls could not rest there (34:14). The text itself proves that "smoke ascending for ever" does not mean perpetual combustion.

**6. Shared brimstone vocabulary between Isaiah 34:9 and Genesis 19:24:**

Both use *gophriyth* (H1614). This shared vocabulary connects the Edom judgment to the Sodom paradigm. The same word, the same imagery, the same result: destructive judgment producing permanent desolation. The vocabulary chain confirms that the fire-and-brimstone imagery operates as a unified paradigm across the OT.

**7. Isaiah 1:31 -- "none shall quench them":**

> "And the strong shall be as tow, and the maker of it as a spark, and they shall both burn together, and none shall quench them." -- Isaiah 1:31

Tow (flax fiber) is one of the most combustible materials available. When burned, it is fully and rapidly consumed. "None shall quench" does not mean the fire burns forever -- it means no one can extinguish it before it finishes its work. Unquenchable fire is irresistible fire, not eternal fire. The tow is consumed; the fire goes out because there is nothing left to burn. This parallels the "not quenched night nor day" language in Isa 34:10.

## Section C: Greek Vocabulary in Revelation 14

**8. *Basanismos* (G929) -- all 6 NT occurrences:**

| Reference | Subject | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Rev 9:5 | Locusts' victims | Torment limited to five months |
| Rev 14:11 | Beast-worshippers | Smoke of torment ascends "for ever and ever" |
| Rev 18:7 | Babylon | "So much torment and sorrow give her" |
| Rev 18:10 | Babylon | Merchants lament "the hour of thy judgment" |
| Rev 18:15 | Babylon | Merchants stand afar off "for the fear of her torment" |

Three of six occurrences (50%) describe Babylon's "torment." But Babylon's torment IS her completed destruction: "she shall be utterly burned with fire" (18:8), judged "in one hour" (18:10), and "thrown down, and shall be found no more at all" (18:21). When the word *basanismos* is used for Babylon, it describes a finished event -- a destruction so complete that the city is "found no more at all." Same word, same book, same author as Rev 14:11.

**9. Rev 9:5 -- basanismos with a time limit:**

Rev 9:5 uses *basanismos* with an explicit time limit: "their torment was as the torment of a scorpion, when he striketh a man... five months." This demonstrates that *basanismos* does not inherently mean "eternal." The word describes the experience of undergoing suffering or distress, and its duration is determined by context, not by the word itself.

**10. *Basanizo* (G928) -- wide semantic range:**

| Reference | Subject | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Matthew 8:6 | A paralytic servant | Physical illness -- "grievously tormented" (suffering from paralysis) |
| Matthew 14:24 | A boat | Waves tossing a ship -- "tossed" (physical battering) |
| Mark 6:48 | Disciples | "Toiling in rowing" -- hard physical labor against wind |
| 2 Peter 2:8 | Lot | Emotional/moral distress -- "vexed his righteous soul" |
| Revelation 12:2 | A woman in labor | Labor pains -- "pained to be delivered" |

The word covers physical illness, waves battering a boat, hard rowing, moral distress, and labor pains. It does not inherently mean "eternal conscious suffering." It describes various forms of distress, pain, and difficulty. The specific nature and duration are determined entirely by context.

**11. *Anapausis* (G372) -- "no rest day and night" in Rev 4:8 vs. Rev 14:11:**

In Rev 4:8, the four living creatures "rest not day and night" (*anapausis*) as they say "Holy, holy, holy." This describes the continuous, uninterrupted quality of their praise during its execution. Nobody reads Rev 4:8 as a statement about eternal duration in the way that Rev 14:11 is read -- the emphasis is on the unceasing nature of the activity. Same word, same book, same author. The Rev 4:8 usage demonstrates that "no rest day and night" characterizes the continuous quality of an activity, not necessarily its infinite duration.

> "And the four beasts had each of them six wings about him; and they were full of eyes within: and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come." -- Revelation 4:8

**12. Rev 14:13 -- the faithful dead "rest":**

> "Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them." -- Revelation 14:13

Two verses after "no rest day nor night" for beast-worshippers, the text describes the faithful dead receiving rest (*anapauomai*, a cognate of *anapausis*). The literary contrast is between those who have "no rest" (14:11) and those who do "rest" (14:13). This antithetical structure characterizes the contrasting states of two groups, using the same word family for emphasis. The passage is structured as a literary unit, not a bare doctrinal proposition.

## Section D: Genre Gate Analysis -- Apocalyptic Vision

**13. Revelation 1:1 and the genre of Revelation:**

> "The Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave unto him, to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass; and he sent and signified [*semaino*] it by his angel unto his servant John." -- Revelation 1:1

The word *semaino* means "to communicate through signs" -- the very first verse tells the reader that the book operates through sign-language, not literal description. Nave's Topical Bible classifies the entire book under VISION, and Rev 14:8-13 is specifically listed under VISION. Reading Rev 14:10-11 as a literal didactic statement about human destiny requires treating apocalyptic vision imagery as if it were didactic prose. In the evidence classification system, didactic prose (Jesus' direct teaching, Paul's letters) is clearer than apocalyptic vision for establishing doctrine. This is a genre consideration, not a dismissal of the text.

**14. Rev 14:10 -- "in the presence of the Lamb":**

The preposition *enopion* ("in the presence of / before") is a judgment and throne-room word throughout Revelation (4:5-6; 7:9; 8:2-4; 20:12). Rev 14:10 describes a public judgment event -- torment occurring "in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb." The same *enopion* is used for the redeemed worshipping before the throne (7:9), which is clearly an event rather than a permanent fixed position. This suggests Rev 14:10 describes a judgment event, not a permanent state.

**15. The "eis tous aionas ton aionon" formula -- distribution and function:**

The "for ever and ever" formula is used approximately 19 times for God/Christ in doxological contexts ("unto him be glory for ever and ever"). It is used 3 times in judgment contexts: Rev 14:11 (beast-worshippers' smoke), Rev 19:3 (Babylon's smoke), and Rev 20:10 (devil, beast, false prophet). When the same formula is applied to Babylon's smoke (Rev 19:3) -- a symbolic entity already described as "utterly burned with fire" (18:8) and "found no more at all" (18:21) -- the formula cannot require literal infinite duration in that instance. Babylon is destroyed. Her smoke ascends "for ever and ever" as a marker of permanent, irreversible judgment -- not ongoing combustion of a city that no longer exists. If the formula can describe completed destruction in Rev 19:3, the same possibility exists in Rev 14:11.

## Section E: Cross-Referencing Rev 14 with OT Judgment Imagery

**16. Psalm 37:20 -- smoke as end product of consumption:**

> "But the wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away." -- Psalm 37:20

Smoke is the end product of the wicked being consumed. As sacrificial fat burns on the altar, it produces smoke that rises and disappears. The substance is consumed; the smoke dissipates; nothing remains. Smoke represents the aftermath of destruction, not evidence of ongoing existence.

**17. Hosea 13:3 and Isaiah 51:6 -- smoke as transience:**

> "Therefore they shall be as the morning cloud, and as the early dew that passeth away, as the chaff that is driven with the whirlwind out of the floor, and as the smoke out of the chimney." -- Hosea 13:3

> "Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look upon the earth beneath: for the heavens shall vanish away like smoke." -- Isaiah 51:6

In Hosea 13:3, smoke is grouped with morning cloud, early dew, and chaff -- four images of things that disappear. In Isaiah 51:6, the heavens themselves "vanish away like smoke." The OT smoke vocabulary consistently represents vanishing, transience, and ceasing to exist -- not permanence or ongoing conscious existence.

**18. Ezekiel 28:18-19 -- fire, ashes, and cessation:**

> "I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth, in the sight of all them that behold thee. All they that know thee among the people shall be astonished at thee: thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more." -- Ezekiel 28:18-19

The sequence is: fire devours -> ashes produced -> "never shalt thou be any more." Fire is the agent. Ashes are the product. Non-existence is the final state. The three elements form a chain: the fire destroys, the ashes are what remains, and the subject ceases to exist. This is the OT fire-judgment pattern: destruction leading to cessation, not ongoing torment.

**19. Nahum 1:8-10 -- utter end:**

> "But with an overrunning flood he will make an utter end of the place thereof... they shall be devoured as stubble fully dry." -- Nahum 1:8,10

"Utter end" (*kalah*) and "devoured as stubble fully dry" describe complete consumption with nothing remaining. Dry stubble burns rapidly and completely. The result is described as an "utter end" -- not a state of ongoing existence.

**20. Psalm 11:6 and the cup imagery in Rev 14:10:**

> "Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup." -- Psalm 11:6

The "cup" language in Rev 14:10 ("the cup of his indignation") draws from OT judgment-cup passages. In the OT, the cup of wrath is a recurring image: Isaiah 51:17,22 (the cup of fury is drunk and drained -- a completed act); Jeremiah 25:15-16 (drink, be mad, fall -- completed); Psalm 75:8 (cup, wring out, drink -- completed). Every OT cup-of-wrath passage describes a finite act of divine judgment that is poured out, drunk, and finished. The cup language frames Rev 14:10 as a judgment event, not an eternal state.

## Section F: Rev 20:9-10 -- Different Subjects, Different Vocabulary

**21. Revelation 20:9-10 comparison:**

| | Rev 20:9 (humans) | Rev 20:10 (devil, beast, false prophet) |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects | Human enemies (Gog and Magog) | The devil, the beast, the false prophet |
| Verb | *katephagen* -- "devoured" | *basanisthesontai* -- "tormented" |
| Duration | Not specified (devoured = completed) | "Day and night for ever and ever" |
| Subject type | Human | Non-human (devil) + symbolic entities (beast, false prophet) |

The text uses destruction vocabulary ("devoured") for human enemies and torment vocabulary ("tormented") for the devil and two symbolic entities -- in adjacent verses. The distinction is the author's own. When Revelation describes what happens to humans in the lake of fire, it uses "devoured" (20:9) and "second death" (20:14-15; 21:8). The "tormented for ever and ever" language in 20:10 is applied to the devil, the beast (a symbolic system), and the false prophet (a symbolic figure).

**22. Symbolic entities and literal human fate:**

The beast in Revelation represents a political/religious system, not a literal animal. The false prophet is likewise a symbolic figure. These are not literal beings who can be "tormented" in the way a conscious person is tormented. Applying the fate of symbolic entities to literal human beings requires an inference: the reader must decide that what is said about symbols should be transferred to what happens to real people. The text itself does not make this transfer. When the text does address humans, it uses "devoured" (20:9) and "second death" (20:14-15; 21:8).

## Section G: Evidence Classification Exercise -- Answers

**1. "Isaiah 34:10 uses the identical 'smoke ascending for ever' and 'not quenched night nor day' formula found in Revelation 14:11, and Isaiah 34:11-17 describes animals inhabiting the desolated ruins."**
- **Explicit (E).** Both facts are directly quotable from the text. The verbal parallel between Isa 34:10 and Rev 14:11 is observable. The animal habitation in Isa 34:11-17 is readable from the passage.

**2. "The 'smoke ascending for ever' formula in its OT source (Isa 34:10) describes permanent desolation, not ongoing combustion, because animals dwell in the ruins (Isa 34:11-17)."**
- **Necessary Implication (N).** If the fire were still burning and the smoke still physically ascending, animals could not dwell in and rest in the territory. The text itself demonstrates that "smoke ascending for ever" and "not quenched night nor day" do not describe perpetual combustion. No interpretation is added -- the passage says both things (smoke for ever, animals dwelling), and the conclusion follows unavoidably.

**3. "Revelation 14:10-11 teaches that all the wicked will experience ongoing, conscious, eternal torment in the afterlife."**
- **Inference (I).** This claim requires: (a) reading apocalyptic vision imagery as literal didactic teaching (the passage is in Revelation's apocalyptic framework); (b) detaching the smoke-ascending formula from its OT source (Isa 34:10), where the same formula describes permanent desolation, not ongoing combustion; (c) reading *basanismos* as "eternal conscious suffering" when same-book usage (Rev 18:7,10,15) describes a completed destruction event; (d) extending the language beyond the passage's stated subjects (beast-worshippers in apocalyptic context) to "all the wicked in the afterlife." Each of these steps adds something beyond what the text says.

**4. "Basanismos (G929) describes Babylon's completed destruction in 3 of its 6 NT occurrences."**
- **Explicit (E).** The distribution data is observable. Rev 18:7,10,15 use *basanismos* for Babylon. Rev 18:8,21 describe Babylon as utterly burned and found no more at all. Three of six occurrences describe a completed destruction. This is textual and distributional fact.

**5. "Because basanismos is used for Babylon's completed destruction in same-book context, it does not require 'eternal conscious suffering' as its meaning."**
- **Necessary Implication (N).** If the same word describes a completed destruction in the same book (Babylon), then the word demonstrably does not require "eternal conscious suffering" as its meaning. Both sides accept the distribution data. The conclusion follows from the data without adding anything.

**6. "The fate described for the devil, beast, and false prophet in Rev 20:10 applies equally to all unsaved humans."**
- **Inference (I).** Rev 20:10 names three subjects: the devil, the beast, and the false prophet. The text does not state that all humans share this fate. The immediately preceding verse (20:9) uses "devoured" for humans. Verses 14-15 use "second death" for humans. The claim adds something the text does not say -- that the fate of these three entities extends to all unsaved humans.

**7. "When humans enter the lake of fire (Rev 20:14-15; 21:8), the identifying term is 'second death.'"**
- **Explicit (E).** Rev 21:8: "the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death." Rev 20:14: "This is the second death." Directly quotable.

## Section H: What CAN Be Said / What CANNOT Be Said -- Answers

**What CAN be said (the Bible explicitly states or necessarily implies):**

1. The "smoke ascending for ever" and "not quenched night nor day" formula in Rev 14:11 draws from Isaiah 34:10, which uses the identical language for Edom's judgment. In the OT source, the same chapter (Isa 34:11-17) describes animals permanently dwelling in the desolated ruins. The fire is not still burning. The formula describes permanent desolation, not ongoing combustion.

2. The fire-and-brimstone paradigm, from its first occurrence (Gen 19:24) through every OT instance and its NT references (Luke 17:29; Jude 1:7; 2 Pet 2:6), describes an act of destructive judgment with a permanent result. Jesus says it "destroyed them all" (Luke 17:29). Peter says it produced "ashes" (2 Pet 2:6).

3. Basanismos (G929) occurs 6 times -- all in Revelation. Three of six describe Babylon's completed destruction. One (Rev 9:5) has a five-month time limit. Same-book, same-word context demonstrates that basanismos does not require "eternal conscious suffering" as its meaning.

4. Anapausis (G372, "rest") in Rev 14:11 uses the same word as Rev 4:8, where living creatures "rest not day and night" in praising God. The Rev 4:8 usage demonstrates that "no rest day and night" characterizes the continuous quality of an activity during its execution.

5. Rev 19:3 uses the identical "smoke for ever and ever" formula as Rev 14:11, but for Babylon, whose destruction is complete and total (Rev 18:8,17,21). Same author, same book, same formula applied to a completed destruction.

6. Rev 20:9 uses destruction vocabulary ("devoured") for human enemies. When humans enter the lake of fire (Rev 20:14-15; 21:8), the identifying term is "second death." The text distinguishes between human subjects and the devil/beast/false prophet.

7. Malachi 4:1-3, the OT's eschatological fire-judgment text, describes the wicked as stubble burned up, leaving neither root nor branch, reduced to ashes underfoot.

8. All primary Revelation passages (Rev 14:9-11; 19:3; 20:10) are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. Rev 1:1 states the book communicates through signs (*semaino*). Nave's classifies Revelation under VISION.

9. OT smoke imagery consistently represents the aftermath of completed destruction (Gen 19:28; Isa 34:10), transience and vanishing (Ps 37:20; Hos 13:3; Isa 51:6), or divine presence (Exo 19:18). No OT usage of smoke describes ongoing conscious existence.

**What CANNOT be said (not stated or necessarily implied -- requires adding something):**

1. It cannot be said that Rev 14:10-11 teaches ongoing eternal conscious torment as a didactic doctrine. The passage is in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. The smoke-ascending formula draws from Isa 34:10, where it describes permanent desolation with animals inhabiting the ruins. Same-book usage: basanismos describes Babylon's completed destruction; the same smoke formula is applied to destroyed Babylon in Rev 19:3.

2. It cannot be said that the "smoke ascending for ever" formula means perpetual combustion. The OT source (Isa 34:10) uses the identical formula for Edom, followed by descriptions of animals dwelling in the ruins. Rev 19:3 uses the same formula for Babylon, already "found no more at all."

3. It cannot be said that basanismos inherently means "eternal conscious suffering." Three of its six occurrences describe Babylon's completed destruction. One has a five-month time limit. The word describes the experience of undergoing judgment, and same-book usage shows it can describe a finite, completed event.

4. It cannot be said that "no rest day nor night" describes an eternal conscious state. The same word (anapausis) in the same book (Rev 4:8) describes the living creatures' continuous praise -- characterizing the uninterrupted nature of the activity, not its eternal duration.

5. It cannot be said that the fate described for the devil, beast, and false prophet in Rev 20:10 applies to all humans. The text names three specific subjects. Rev 20:9 uses "devoured" for humans. Rev 20:14-15 and 21:8 use "second death" for humans. Extending Rev 20:10 to all humans is an inference that the text does not make.

6. It cannot be said that fire-and-brimstone imagery describes ongoing conscious torment rather than destruction. The paradigm, from Gen 19 through every OT occurrence, describes destructive judgment. Jesus says it "destroyed them all" (Luke 17:29). Peter says it produced "ashes" (2 Pet 2:6). Jude calls it an "example" -- and Sodom is not still burning.
