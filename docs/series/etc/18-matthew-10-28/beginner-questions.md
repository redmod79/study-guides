# Study 18: Matthew 10:28

*"Destroy Both Soul and Body in Hell" -- What Does Jesus Mean?*

---

> **A Note on How to Study**
>
> Throughout this series, we distinguish between what the Bible **explicitly says** and what people **infer** from the Bible. These are not the same thing.
>
> - **Explicit:** You can point to a verse and quote it. The text directly says this.
> - **Inference:** Someone claims the Bible teaches something, but no single verse says it. They have drawn a conclusion -- possibly a good one, possibly not -- but the conclusion itself is not in the text.
>
> In this study, we will examine Matthew 10:28 in detail, paying close attention to how Matthew himself uses the key words in this verse throughout his Gospel. Same-author usage is one of the most reliable tools for determining what a word means in a specific context.

---

## Section A: The Verse Itself

1. Read **Matthew 10:28**. What two things does Jesus contrast in this verse? What can men do, and what can God do?

2. What word does Jesus use for what God does to "both soul and body in hell" -- does He say "torment" or "destroy"?

3. Jesus says men can kill the body but are "not able to kill the soul." Does this verse say the soul is *immortal* or *indestructible*? What does it say God can do to the soul?

---

## Section B: How Matthew Uses "Soul" (Psyche, G5590)

4. Read **Matthew 10:39**. This verse is only 11 verses after 10:28, in the same discourse. What word is translated "life" here? (Hint: it is the same Greek word *psyche* that is translated "soul" in 10:28.) What does "lose his life" mean in this verse?

5. Read **Matthew 16:25-26**. In verse 25, *psyche* is translated "life." In verse 26, the same Greek word is translated "soul." What does this tell you about the relationship between "life" and "soul" in Matthew's vocabulary?

6. Read **Matthew 2:20** and **Matthew 6:25**. In both verses, *psyche* is translated "life." What kind of "life" is being discussed -- physical life, or an immaterial soul?

7. Looking at all these passages together (10:28, 10:39, 16:25-26, 2:20, 6:25), does Matthew use *psyche* to mean "the whole living self/person" or exclusively "an immaterial soul that survives death"?

---

## Section C: How Matthew Uses "Destroy" (Apollymi, G622)

8. Read **Matthew 10:39** again. What word is translated "lose" in "he that findeth his life shall lose it"? (It is the same Greek word *apollymi* that is translated "destroy" in 10:28.) What does this tell you about the word pair *psyche* + *apollymi* in Matthew?

9. Read **Matthew 21:41**. In this parable, what does the vineyard lord do to the wicked tenants? What word is used? Does it mean "torment" or "destroy"?

10. Read **Matthew 22:7**. What does the king do to the murderers who rejected his invitation? What word is used?

11. Read **Matthew 5:29-30**. What does Jesus say is better than having your "whole body cast into hell"? What word does He use for what happens to the member that is cut off? (Hint: it is *apollymi* -- "perish.")

---

## Section D: How Matthew Uses "Hell" (Gehenna, G1067)

12. Matthew uses the word *gehenna* (translated "hell") 7 times -- more than any other NT author. Read **Matthew 5:22**, **5:29-30**, **10:28**, **18:9**, and **23:33**. In any of these passages, does Jesus pair gehenna with the word "torment"? What kinds of words does He pair with gehenna instead?

13. Read **Matthew 18:9**. What is the alternative to being "cast into hell fire"? What does the contrast between "enter into life" and "cast into hell fire" suggest about what gehenna represents?

---

## Section E: The Synoptic Parallel

14. Read **Luke 12:4-5**, which records the same teaching as Matthew 10:28. What word does Luke use for what God does -- "destroy" or "kill"? Does Luke mention the "soul" at all?

15. Luke 12:5 describes a two-step sequence: God kills, and then has power to cast into gehenna. What does this sequence suggest about what happens in gehenna -- is it a place of ongoing torment for the living, or a place where the already-dead are cast?

16. Comparing Matthew 10:28 and Luke 12:4-5, both authors agree on two things about gehenna. What are they? (Hint: think about who has authority over gehenna, and what vocabulary is absent from both accounts.)
