# Study 12: Tormented Forever -- Advanced Study

*Revelation 20:10 -- Subject Identification, Basanizo Semantic Range, and Vocabulary Differentiation*

---

> **Evidence Tiers**
>
> This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** -- The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** -- No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed -- every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
> 3. **Inference (I)** -- Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be *added* beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.
>
> The hierarchy matters: **Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference.** An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

## Section A: Subject Gate Analysis -- Who Are the Three Entities?

1. Revelation 20:10 names three subjects for the "tormented day and night for ever and ever" formula: the devil, the beast, and the false prophet. For each, document its nature from the text:
   - **The devil:** Read **Revelation 12:9** and **20:2**. What is the devil identified as? Is this entity a human being?
   - **The beast:** Read **Revelation 13:1-2** and **17:8**. What characteristics does the text assign to the beast (origin, appearance, description)? Could these characteristics apply to a literal human person?
   - **The false prophet:** Read **Revelation 19:20** and trace the identification back to **Revelation 13:11-17**. The Greek word *pseudoprophetes* appears 3 times in Revelation (16:13; 19:20; 20:10) as a singular specific figure, and 8 times elsewhere in the NT as a generic plural class of human beings. What is the significance of this distinction?

2. Apply the Subject Gate to each entity in Rev 20:10. For each subject, determine: Is this (a) a literal human being, (b) a non-human supernatural spirit being, or (c) a symbolic entity in an apocalyptic vision? What does the gate result mean for using this verse as evidence about the fate of literal human beings?

3. Read **Revelation 1:1**. The Greek word *semaino* (to signify, to communicate through signs) describes how the book's content is delivered. Read also the classification in Nave's Topical Bible, which lists Revelation under VISION. What does the genre classification mean for reading Rev 20:10 as literal didactic teaching about human eschatology?

## Section D: The "Ages of Ages" Formula

11. The Greek phrase *eis tous aionas ton aionon* ("for ever and ever" / "unto the ages of the ages") appears approximately 22 times in the NT. Document its distribution:
    - How many times is it used doxologically (for God/Christ's glory, reign, etc.)?
    - How many times is it used in judgment contexts?
    - Where do the judgment uses appear (which book)?

12. Read **Revelation 19:3**. The "smoke rose up for ever and ever" formula is applied to Babylon. Now read **Revelation 18:21**: Babylon is "thrown down, and shall be found no more at all." If the same "for ever and ever" formula describes the smoke of an entity that is "found no more at all," what does this tell you about how the formula functions in Revelation's apocalyptic framework?

## Section E: The Extension Argument

13. The most common argument for applying Rev 20:10 to humans is: "The devil, beast, and false prophet are tormented in the lake of fire. Humans are also cast into the lake of fire (Rev 20:15). Therefore humans experience the same torment." Evaluate this argument:
    - Does the text state that all inhabitants of the lake share the same experience?
    - What vocabulary does the text use when it describes human beings entering the lake?
    - If "same destination = same experience," why does the text use different vocabulary for different subjects at the same destination?

14. A related argument holds that the beast represents a human institution, so its torment transfers to its human referents. Evaluate this claim against the text's vocabulary distinctions:
    - The symbolic trio (devil, beast, false prophet) receives which vocabulary in Rev 20:10?
    - Human beings receive which vocabulary in Rev 19:21, 20:9, and 20:14-15?
    - The text defines the lake of fire as ___ (Rev 20:14) -- whose own self-interpretation is this?

15. Read **Matthew 25:41** and **Matthew 25:46**. The everlasting fire was "prepared for the devil and his angels." The punishment vocabulary for humans in Matt 25:46 is *kolasis* (G2851, "punishment"), not *basanizo* (G928, "torment"). *Kolasis* appears only twice in the NT: Matt 25:46 and 1 John 4:18. What is the significance of the vocabulary difference between the fire's preparation (for the devil) and the human punishment term (*kolasis*)?
