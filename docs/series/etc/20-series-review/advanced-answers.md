## Section A: Anthropology and Immortality (Studies 1-2)

**1. List the three components of Genesis 2:7's creation formula. What Hebrew parsing confirms man *became* (not *received*) a soul?**

Dust of the ground + breath of life (*neshamah*) = living soul (*nephesh chayyah*). The verb *hayah* is Qal Wayyiqtol 3ms — "and he became." The grammar does not support "received a soul as a separate component."

**2. How does *nephesh*'s use for dead bodies and blood affect the "immortal soul" claim?**

*Nephesh meth* ("dead soul") in Lev 21:11 and Num 6:6 shows that Scripture itself calls a corpse a "dead soul." Lev 17:11 identifies the *nephesh* with the blood — a physical substance. An immortal, immaterial entity cannot be "in the blood" or be "dead." These usages demonstrate that *nephesh* refers to the whole living being, not an immortal component.

**3. Three Greek immortality words:**

- *Athanasia* (G110, 3 occurrences): God alone possesses it (1 Tim 6:16). Believers put it on at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54). Never applied to the human soul.
- *Aphtharsia* (G861, 8 occurrences): Must be sought (Rom 2:7). Brought to light through the gospel (2 Tim 1:10). The resurrection body is raised in *aphtharsia* (1 Cor 15:42).
- *Thnetos* (G2349, 6 occurrences): Describes the current human body as mortal (Rom 6:12; 2 Cor 4:11). Must be transformed (1 Cor 15:53; 2 Cor 5:4).

**4. What E-items must the "immortal soul" claim override?**

- E016 (Ezek 18:4,20): "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" — *mut* applied to *nephesh*
- E028 (Matt 10:28): God "is able to destroy both soul and body in gehenna" — *apollymi* applied to *psyche*
- E024 (1 Tim 6:16): God "only hath immortality" — *monos* + *athanasia*
- E011 (Ps 146:4): "In that very day his thoughts perish" — cessation at death
- E019 (Ecc 9:5): "The dead know not any thing" — no consciousness

This is why the claim is classified I-D: it requires overriding multiple explicit statements, redefining "die," "destroy," and "only."

## Section B: Death and the Dead (Studies 3-4)

**5. Five passages from three+ authors describing the dead as unconscious:**

1. Psalm 146:4 (Psalmist): "In that very day his thoughts perish"
2. Ecclesiastes 9:5 (Solomon): "The dead know not any thing"
3. Ecclesiastes 9:10 (Solomon): "No work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave"
4. Psalm 6:5 (David): "In death there is no remembrance of thee"
5. Job 14:21 (Job): "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not"

Additional authors: Isaiah (38:18-19), Hezekiah's words recorded by Isaiah. At least eight OT authors make these statements.

**6. Two alleged intermediate-state passages and their interpretive requirements:**

- **2 Cor 5:8** ("absent from the body, present with the Lord"): Paul expressly says he does NOT want to be "unclothed" (disembodied) but "clothed upon" (v.4). The phrase "mortality swallowed up of life" matches 1 Cor 15:54 — describing resurrection, not an intermediate state. The inference requires *choosing* Paul's preference statement over his own didactic teaching in 1 Thess 4:16-17. (I-B)

- **Luke 23:43** ("today shalt thou be with me in paradise"): Greek has no punctuation. The comma placement is editorial, not textual. John 20:17 states Jesus had not yet ascended on Sunday morning. The inference requires *choosing* one punctuation over another, and the chosen reading creates a contradiction with John 20:17. (I-B)

**7. SIS resolution of Phil 1:23 vs. 1 Thess 4:16-17:**

1 Thess 4:16-17 is didactic instruction directly addressing the topic: "The dead in Christ shall rise first: then we... shall be caught up together... and so shall we ever be with the Lord." Phil 1:23 is a personal expression of desire in a single verse. The didactic passage governs the reading of the ambiguous expression. Paul's own teaching ties being with the Lord to the resurrection, not to death. Resolution: Strong — plain didactic statement on one side, personal preference expression on the other.

## Section C: Vocabulary (Studies 5-8)

**8. Four "hell" words:**

- **Sheol** (H7585, 67 occurrences): The grave / abode of all dead. Characterized by silence (Ps 115:17), unconsciousness (Ecc 9:10), darkness (Job 10:21-22). Both righteous and wicked go there.
- **Hades** (G86, 11 occurrences): Greek equivalent of Sheol. LXX translates Sheol as Hades in 58/65 occurrences. Same characteristics.
- **Gehenna** (G1067, 12 occurrences): Eschatological destruction. Paired with *apollymi* (Matt 10:28), never with *basanizo*. Named after the Valley of Hinnom.
- **Tartaroo** (G5020, 1 occurrence): 2 Peter 2:4 — temporary prison for fallen angels. Not applied to humans.

**9. Destruction vs. torment vocabulary:**

No lexicon defines *abad*, *apollumi*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *olethros*, or *apoleia* as "torment." These are destruction/cessation words. The torment vocabulary is a separate word family: *basanizo/basanismos/basanos* (G928/G929/G931). The torment family is never applied to the final fate of generic human wicked in didactic genre — only in apocalyptic Revelation (and there, only to non-human/symbolic subjects in Rev 20:10).

**10. *Olam* and the "endless" assumption:**

The pattern demonstrates that *olam* means "for the duration of the subject" — not inherently "endless." When applied to mortal institutions (priesthood, temple, circumcision), it lasts as long as the institution. When applied to a slave (Exo 21:6), it means until death. When applied to Jonah (Jon 2:6), it means three days. Two *olam* promises were explicitly revoked by God himself. The word's duration is determined by the nature of what it modifies, not by the word itself.

**11. *Aionios* vs. *aidios* and Matt 25:46:**

If *aionios* inherently and always meant "endless/eternal," there would be no need for *aidios* (G126), a separate Greek word meaning "everduring" (used in Rom 1:20 and Jude 6). The existence of *aidios* shows that *aionios* does not by itself convey absolute endlessness — otherwise the language would be redundant. In Matt 25:46, *aionios* modifies both "punishment" (*kolasis*) and "life." The duration of each is determined by its subject: life, being divine in nature, is endless; punishment (*kolasis* — a word associated with capital punishment in LXX usage), being applied to mortal beings, is permanent in result but not necessarily ongoing in process.

## Section D: ECT Proof-Texts (Studies 9-15)

**12. Rich Man and Lazarus — two failed gates:**

- **Gate 1 (Subject):** The characters are allegorical figures in a parable, not literal historical persons. "A certain rich man" uses the same formulaic introduction as Luke 12:16 and 16:1.
- **Gate 3 (Genre):** The passage is parabolic (fictional narrative illustrating a point), not didactic. It is situated within a continuous parabolic discourse (Luke 15:3-16:31). Parabolic genre carries less evidential weight than didactic teaching.

**13. Revelation 20:10 — three subjects:**

1. The devil — a non-human spirit being
2. The beast — a symbolic entity from apocalyptic vision (Rev 13)
3. The false prophet — a symbolic entity from apocalyptic vision (Rev 13)

None is a generic human being. The "tormented day and night for ever and ever" formula appears exactly once in Scripture and is never applied to humans. When humans enter the lake of fire (Rev 20:14-15; 21:8), the text uses different vocabulary: "second death" — death, not torment.

**14. Isaiah 34:9-10 and Edom:**

Edom is not still burning. It has been desolate ruins for millennia. The "smoke ascending forever" formula in Isaiah 34 describes the *permanence and completeness* of the destruction, not ongoing combustion. Animals dwell in the ruins (Isa 34:11-15) — impossible if the fire were still burning. Jude 7 and 2 Peter 2:6 confirm the same pattern for Sodom: "suffering the vengeance of eternal fire" and "turning... into ashes" — the fire is "eternal" in its effect (permanent destruction), not in its duration (still burning).

**15. The structural asymmetry:**

Across 632 unique evidence items classified in 19 studies: Conditionalist evidence includes 107 E-items and 14 N-items (82.3% at E/N tier). ECT evidence includes 0 E-items and 0 N-items (100% at inference level). This means the conditionalist position is built on what the text directly says, while the ECT position is built entirely on interpretive inferences that require adding concepts, choosing contested readings, importing philosophical frameworks, or redefining biblical vocabulary.

## Section E: Context (Studies 16-17)

**16. Historical path of the "immortal soul" concept:**

Plato (*Phaedo*, c. 360 BC) → Hellenistic Judaism (Wisdom of Solomon, Philo) → Alexandrian Christian writers (Clement of Alexandria, Origen) → Augustine (c. 430 AD, former Neoplatonist, synthesized ECT doctrine) → Fifth Lateran Council (1513, declared soul immortality dogma). The earliest Christian writers (apostolic fathers: Clement of Rome, Ignatius, Irenaeus, Justin Martyr, Theophilus) held conditional immortality.

**17. The Anselmian argument — why I-C?**

It is classified I-C (Compatible External) because: (1) No biblical text contains the reasoning "sin against an infinite God requires infinite punishment." (2) It is a philosophical argument originating with Anselm of Canterbury (c. 1098 AD). (3) It contradicts explicit proportionality statements: "according to deeds" (Rom 2:6; Ps 62:12; Job 34:11 — 11+ passages, 9+ authors), "many stripes" vs. "few stripes" (Luke 12:47-48), "more tolerable" for some cities (Matt 10:15), "just recompense" (Heb 2:2). Infinite punishment for finite deeds violates the "according to deeds" principle.

## Section F: Same-Author and Parabolic Analysis (Studies 18-19)

**18. Matthew's *apollymi* pattern:**

When Matthew uses *apollymi* for divine judgment against the wicked — Matt 10:28 (destroy soul and body), 21:41 (miserably destroy those wicked husbandmen), 22:7 (destroyed those murderers and burned their city) — it consistently means actual destruction, not preservation in torment. The same word pair (*psyche* + *apollymi*) in Matt 10:39 means "lose one's life." Matthew never pairs gehenna with *basanizo*.

**19. Parabolic vocabulary pattern:**

Jesus uses *katakaio* ("burn up completely") in the wheat and tares explanation (Matt 13:40) — a word whose *kata-* prefix intensifies total consumption. All 12 NT uses of *katakaio* describe complete burning. "Gnashing of teeth" (*brygmos*) expresses hostile rage in every OT occurrence and in Acts 7:54 (the Sanhedrin gnashing at Stephen in fury). Luke 13:28 identifies the trigger as seeing others in the kingdom while being excluded — grief and rage at exclusion, not physical pain from torment. Building a torment doctrine from parables requires (1) treating parabolic imagery as didactic teaching (genre violation), (2) ignoring that the vocabulary used is destruction, not torment, and (3) reinterpreting rage as pain.

## Master Evidence Classification Exercise — Answers

**1. "The wages of sin is death" (Rom 6:23)**
- **Explicit (E).** Romans 6:23 directly states this.

**2. "The soul is inherently immortal and survives death consciously"**
- **I-D (Counter-Evidence External).** No verse says this. Requires overriding "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezek 18:4), "God is able to destroy both soul and body" (Matt 10:28), "God only hath immortality" (1 Tim 6:16), "the dead know not any thing" (Ecc 9:5), and "his thoughts perish" (Ps 146:4). The concept originates with Plato, not Scripture.

**3. "The wicked are destroyed and cease to exist"**
- **I-A (Evidence-Extending).** All components are drawn from E/N statements (Ezek 18:4, Matt 10:28, Ps 37:20, Mal 4:1-3, 2 Thess 1:9, Rom 6:23, John 3:16). It is an inference only because it systematizes multiple texts into a comprehensive doctrinal position. No concept from outside the text is added. Strongest inference type.

**4. "The wicked will be tormented consciously forever"**
- **I-D (Counter-Evidence External).** No didactic verse says this. Requires: reading apocalyptic imagery as doctrine (genre violation), extending non-human subjects (devil, beast, false prophet) to humans (subject violation), assuming "forever" means endless despite dozens of biblical counter-examples where *olam*/*aionios* describe things that clearly ended, importing the Platonic concept of an immortal soul the text never teaches, and redefining destruction vocabulary (*apollymi*, *olethros*) to mean something other than destruction.

**5. "God alone has immortality; therefore no one else inherently has it"**
- **Necessary Implication (N).** "Only" (*monos*) means exclusively. If God *only* has immortality (1 Tim 6:16 — explicit), then no one else inherently has it. This is what "only" means. Every reader, regardless of theological position, must acknowledge this.

**6. "Rev 20:10's torment formula applies to all wicked humans"**
- **I-C (Compatible External).** The text names three subjects: devil, beast, false prophet. The claim that "same destination = same experience" adds a principle the text does not state. When humans enter the lake of fire, the text uses different vocabulary ("second death"). The extension from three named entities to "all wicked humans" is not in the text.

**7. "The 'forever' language means the destruction is permanent and irreversible"**
- **I-A (Evidence-Extending).** Based on the demonstrated pattern: *olam*/*aionios* describe the permanence of the result (Sodom's eternal fire = permanent ashes; Edom's ascending smoke = permanent desolation; slave's "forever" = until death). Systematizes the textual evidence without adding outside concepts.

**8. "Sin against an infinite God requires infinite punishment"**
- **I-C (Compatible External).** No verse contains this reasoning. It originates with Anselm (c. 1098 AD). It does not directly contradict any E-item (it's a philosophical argument about justice), but it conflicts with the proportionality principle stated in 11+ passages. External framework not derived from Scripture.

**9. "The dead know nothing and are unconscious until resurrection"**
- **I-A (Evidence-Extending).** Systematizes seven explicit statements from five+ authors (Ecc 9:5,10; Ps 6:5; 115:17; 146:4; Job 14:12,21) plus the "sleep" metaphor from 7+ authors. All components from E/N tables. Only an inference because it draws a comprehensive conclusion from multiple texts.

**10. "The Rich Man and Lazarus teaches literal afterlife geography"**
- **I-C (Compatible External).** The passage is parabolic (fails genre gate), the characters are allegorical (fails subject gate), the imagery (Abraham's bosom, conversation across a gulf, dipping a finger in water) is found nowhere else in Scripture, and the passage's own stated teaching point is "they have Moses and the prophets" (v.29-31). Reading it as literal afterlife teaching requires importing a framework the text does not prescribe.

## What CAN Be Said / What CANNOT Be Said — Complete Answers

**What CAN be said (E/N tier across the series):**

1. Man is mortal. Formed from dust, returns to dust (Gen 2:7; 3:19; Ecc 12:7).
2. God alone has immortality (1 Tim 6:16). Humans seek it (Rom 2:7) and receive it at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54).
3. The soul can die (Ezek 18:4) and be destroyed (Matt 10:28).
4. The dead are unconscious — know nothing, thoughts perish, no activity (Ecc 9:5,10; Ps 146:4).
5. The wages of sin is death (Rom 6:23). The alternative to eternal life is perishing (John 3:16).
6. The Bible's vocabulary for the wicked's fate is destruction/cessation — used hundreds of times, never defined as torment.
7. No "hell" word inherently means eternal torment. Gehenna is never paired with *basanizo*.
8. *Olam*/*aionios* do not inherently mean endless — dozens of verifiable finite uses, two *olam* promises explicitly revoked.
9. The "tormented forever" formula (Rev 20:10) is applied to three non-human/symbolic entities, never to generic humans. When humans enter the lake of fire, the text says "second death."
10. Biblical punishment is proportional — "according to deeds" (11+ passages). God does not retain anger forever (Ps 103:9; Mic 7:18).
11. Every recorded divine judgment in Scripture results in death/destruction, not ongoing torment.
12. Death is called "sleep" by 7+ biblical authors. Being with the Lord is tied to resurrection (1 Thess 4:16-17).

**What CANNOT be said (requires inference — no E or N support):**

1. That any explicit biblical statement teaches eternal conscious torment of human beings (0 E-items, 0 N-items in 632 classified).
2. That the soul is inherently immortal (no verse says this; the concept traces to Plato).
3. That "hell" means eternal torment (no word translated "hell" inherently carries this meaning).
4. That destruction vocabulary means "ongoing conscious ruin" (no lexicon supports this).
5. That "forever" always means endless (dozens of counter-examples).
6. That the "tormented forever" formula applies to humans (the text names three non-human/symbolic subjects).
7. That the Rich Man and Lazarus teaches literal afterlife geography (parabolic genre, allegorical characters).
8. That "sin against infinite God requires infinite punishment" is a biblical argument (Anselm, 1098 AD).
9. That ECT can be derived from biblical teaching alone (requires Platonic soul immortality, not found in Scripture).
10. That infinite torment is consistent with the "according to deeds" principle (infinite punishment for finite deeds contradicts explicit proportionality statements).
