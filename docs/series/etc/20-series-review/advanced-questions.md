# Study 20: Series Review — Advanced

*Evidence Classification Across All 19 Studies*

---

> **Evidence Tiers — Final Review**
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** — The text directly says this. You can quote a verse.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** — Unavoidably follows from what the text says. No interpretation needed.
> 3. **Inference (I)** — A claim about what the Bible teaches that no verse says and no combination forces. Something must be added.
>    - **I-A:** Systematizes the text's own words — strongest inference
>    - **I-B:** Competing textual evidence on both sides — resolved by Scripture-interprets-Scripture
>    - **I-C:** Compatible external framework — adds concepts the text doesn't contain but doesn't contradict it
>    - **I-D:** Counter-evidence external — requires overriding what the text explicitly says — weakest inference

## Section A: Anthropology and Immortality (Studies 1-2)

1. List the three components of Genesis 2:7's creation formula. What Hebrew parsing confirms man *became* (not *received*) a soul?

2. *Nephesh* appears ~780 times. How does its use for dead bodies (*nephesh meth*, Lev 21:11) and its identification with blood (Lev 17:11) affect the claim that *nephesh* means "immortal soul"?

3. Three Greek words define biblical immortality: *athanasia* (G110), *aphtharsia* (G861), *thnetos* (G2349). For each, state who possesses it and when believers receive it.

4. The claim "humans possess an immortal soul" was classified I-D in the etc6 series. What explicit statements must it override? (List at least three E-items.)

## Section B: Death and the Dead (Studies 3-4)

5. How many OT authors describe the dead as unconscious? List five passages from at least three different authors.

6. The alleged conscious intermediate state passages (2 Cor 5:8, Phil 1:23, Luke 23:43, Luke 16:19-31, Rev 6:9-11) were each classified as inferences. For any two, identify what interpretive move is required beyond what the text says.

## Section C: Vocabulary (Studies 5-8)

8. No word translated "hell" inherently means eternal torment. For each of the four (Sheol, Hades, Gehenna, Tartaroo), state its actual semantic range and how many times it appears.

9. The Bible's destruction vocabulary (*abad/apollumi*, *shamad*, *kalah*, *olethros*, *apoleia*) appears hundreds of times. Does any lexicon define any of these words as "torment"? What vocabulary family *does* mean torment?

10. *Olam* (H5769) was used for institutions that ended, human lifespans, and three-day periods. Two *olam* promises were explicitly revoked (1 Sam 2:30; 13:13). What does this pattern demonstrate about whether *olam* inherently means "endless"?

11. *Aionios* (G166) is used for past time in three passages (Rom 16:25; 2 Tim 1:9; Tit 1:2). Greek has a separate word, *aidios* (G126), that means "everduring." Why is this distinction significant for interpreting Matt 25:46?

## Section D: ECT Proof-Texts (Studies 9-15)

12. The Rich Man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19-31) fails two validation gates. Which two, and why?

13. In Revelation 20:10, the "tormented forever" formula is applied to three subjects. Name them and classify each (human, non-human, symbolic). Is this formula ever applied to generic human beings anywhere in Scripture?

14. The "smoke ascending forever" imagery (Rev 14:11) is traced to Isaiah 34:9-10 (Edom). What is the current state of Edom — still burning, or desolate ruins? What does this tell you about the formula?

15. Study 15 evaluated the eight strongest ECT arguments. The result: 0 E-items, 0 N-items, 12 ECT inference items (4 I-B, 7 I-C, 1 I-D). What does this structural asymmetry mean for the evidentiary basis of the ECT position?

## Section F: Same-Author and Parabolic Analysis (Studies 18-19)

18. In Matthew's Gospel, when *apollymi* is used for divine judgment (10:28; 21:41; 22:7), does it mean torment or destruction? When the same word pair (*psyche* + *apollymi*) appears in Matt 10:39, what does it mean?
