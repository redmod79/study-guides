# Study 16: Where Did the Doctrine of Eternal Torment Come From?

*A Historical Investigation*

---

> **A Note on This Study**
>
> This study is different from the others in the series. It is historical, not doctrinal -- it traces *how* the doctrine of eternal conscious torment (ECT) entered Christian theology.
>
> The explicit/inference distinction still applies, but the question changes slightly:
>
> - **Explicit:** Does the Bible directly say this?
> - **Inference:** Does a philosopher, church father, or church council say this -- and is it being treated as though the Bible said it?
>
> Understanding where an idea came from does not automatically make it wrong. But it does matter whether a doctrine originates from Scripture or from somewhere else.

---

## Section A: What Does the Bible Say About the Soul?

1. Read **Genesis 2:7**. God formed man from dust and breathed into him the breath of life, and man "became a living soul." Did man *receive* a soul as a separate thing, or did man *become* a living soul?

2. Read **1 Timothy 6:16**. Who "only hath immortality"? According to this verse, is immortality something all humans naturally possess, or something that belongs to God alone?

3. Read **Romans 2:7**. Paul describes people who "seek for glory and honour and immortality." If humans already had immortal souls, would they need to *seek* immortality?

4. Read **Ezekiel 18:4**. "The soul that sinneth, it shall ___." Can a soul die, according to this verse?

5. Read **Matthew 10:28**. Jesus says to fear God, who is able to "destroy both soul and body in hell." If the soul were indestructible (as Plato taught), could God destroy it?

---

## Section B: Where Did the "Immortal Soul" Idea Come From?

6. The Greek philosopher Plato (c. 428-348 BC) wrote a dialogue called the *Phaedo* in which he argued that the soul is inherently immortal, indestructible, and pre-existent. According to Plato, the body is a prison and death liberates the soul. Compare this with Genesis 2:7 and Ecclesiastes 9:5 ("the dead know not any thing"). Do Plato's claims match what the Bible says about the soul?

7. Read **Acts 17:18,32**. When Paul preached the resurrection to Greek philosophers in Athens, what was their reaction? Why would resurrection be "strange" to someone who already believed the soul was immortal?

8. Read **Colossians 2:8**. Paul warns: "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men." What kind of influence is Paul warning against?

---

## Section C: How Did the Idea Enter Christianity?

9. The earliest Christian writers after the apostles (called the Apostolic Fathers, c. 90-150 AD) taught that immortality is a gift for the redeemed -- not something all humans have by nature. Clement of Rome called it a "gift." Ignatius called the Eucharist "the medicine of immortality." Theophilus of Antioch said man was made "capable of both" immortality and death, with immortality as a reward. If the earliest Christians taught conditional immortality, what does this suggest about whether ECT was part of original Christian teaching?

10. The first Christian writer to teach that the soul is naturally immortal was Athenagoras (c. 177 AD), who was a former Platonist philosopher. Later, Clement of Alexandria (c. 200 AD) explicitly set out to merge Greek philosophy with Christian teaching, and his student Origen (c. 230 AD) taught the pre-existence of souls -- an idea directly from Plato. What is the common thread among the early Christians who introduced soul immortality into the church?

11. Augustine of Hippo (c. 430 AD) was a former Neoplatonist -- a follower of a philosophical tradition descended from Plato. He combined Platonic soul immortality with eternal punishment to produce the systematic ECT doctrine that dominated Western Christianity. How many years after the apostles did Augustine write?

---

## Section D: When Did It Become Official Doctrine?

12. The Fourth Lateran Council (1215 AD) formalized eternal torment as official church doctrine -- approximately 1,200 years after the apostolic period. The Fifth Lateran Council (1513 AD) declared it heretical to teach "that the soul is mortal" -- 1,513 years after Christ. Meanwhile, no church council ever condemned conditional immortality (the view that immortality is a gift, not inherent). Only universalism (everyone is saved) was condemned (553 AD). What do these dates tell you about whether ECT was part of the original Christian faith?

13. The ECT doctrine requires two premises: (1) the soul is inherently immortal, and (2) God punishes the wicked eternally. If premise (1) -- the soul is inherently immortal -- came from Plato rather than from Scripture, what happens to premise (2)?
