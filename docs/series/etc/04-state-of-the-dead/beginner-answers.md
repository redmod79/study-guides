# Study 4: State of the Dead — Conscious or Unconscious? — Answer Key

*What Does the Bible Say About the Condition of the Dead Between Death and Resurrection?*

---

> **A Note on How to Study**
>
> In Study 1, we learned to distinguish between what the Bible **explicitly says** and what people **infer** from the Bible. These are not the same thing.
>
> - **Explicit:** You can point to a verse and quote it. The text directly says this.
> - **Inference:** Someone claims the Bible teaches something, but no single verse says it. They have drawn a conclusion — possibly a good one, possibly not — but the conclusion itself is not in the text.
>
> This distinction will be especially important in this study. Many popular beliefs about what happens to the dead come from inferences, not from explicit statements. We will look at every passage — including the ones people use to argue for consciousness after death — and ask: what does the text actually say?

---

## Section A: What Does the Old Testament Say About the Dead?

**1. Read Psalm 146:4. When a person dies and their breath departs, what happens to their thoughts? Does the text say this is gradual or immediate ("in that very day")?**

Their thoughts perish — and this happens "in that very day." The cessation of thought is immediate at death, not gradual.

> "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish." — Psalm 146:4

**2. Read Ecclesiastes 9:5-6. What do the dead know? What has happened to their love, hatred, and envy?**

The dead know nothing at all. Their love, hatred, and envy have perished. They have "no more a portion for ever in any thing that is done under the sun."

> "For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten. Also their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished." — Ecclesiastes 9:5-6

**3. Read Ecclesiastes 9:10. What work, knowledge, or wisdom exists in the grave (sheol)?**

None. "There is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest." Sheol is characterized by the total absence of activity and awareness.

**4. Read Psalm 6:5 and Psalm 115:17. Do the dead praise God? Is there remembrance of God in death? Where do the dead go?**

No. In death there is no remembrance of God, and no one gives thanks. "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence" (Ps 115:17). The dead go "into silence."

> "In death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?" — Psalm 6:5

**5. Read Job 14:10-12, 21. What happens to a man when he dies? Does he know what happens to his children?**

Man "lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more, they shall not awake, nor be raised out of their sleep" (v.12). He does not know what happens to his children: "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not of them" (v.21). The dead are completely unaware of events among the living.

## Section B: What Did Jesus and the Apostles Call Death?

**6. Read John 11:11-14. What did Jesus call Lazarus' condition? Then what did He clarify? Why is the word Jesus chose significant?**

Jesus said "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth" — then clarified plainly: "Lazarus is dead." Jesus deliberately equated death with sleep. This is significant because sleep is a state of unconsciousness, rest, and future awakening. If the dead were actually conscious in another realm, calling death "sleep" would be misleading.

> "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep... Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead." — John 11:11, 14

**7. Read Matthew 9:24 (also Mark 5:39 and Luke 8:52). What did Jesus say about the dead girl? How did the mourners react?**

Jesus said "the maid is not dead, but sleepeth." The mourners "laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead" (Luke 8:53). Jesus chose the word "sleep" while fully knowing the girl was dead. He used this term in three separate Gospel accounts.

**8. Read Acts 7:60 and Acts 13:36. What language did Luke use for Stephen's death? What language did Paul use for David's death?**

Luke says Stephen "fell asleep" (Acts 7:60). Paul says David "fell on sleep, and was laid unto his fathers, and saw corruption" (Acts 13:36). Both use sleep language for death, consistent with Jesus' usage.

**9. Read 1 Corinthians 15:6, 18, 51 and 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14. What is Paul's standard term for the Christian dead? How many times does he use sleep language in these passages alone?**

Paul uses *koimao* (sleep) as his standard term: "fallen asleep" (1 Cor 15:6), "asleep in Christ" (1 Cor 15:18), "we shall not all sleep" (1 Cor 15:51), "them which are asleep" (1 Thess 4:13), "sleep in Jesus" (1 Thess 4:14). At least five uses of sleep language in these four passages alone. This was his consistent terminology, not an occasional metaphor.

## Section C: What About Passages That Seem to Teach Consciousness After Death?

**10. Read 2 Corinthians 5:4, 8 and 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17.**

Paul says he is willing to be "absent from the body and present with the Lord" (v.8). But in verse 4, Paul explicitly says he does NOT want to be "unclothed" (disembodied) — he wants to be "clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life." This phrase ("mortality swallowed up") matches 1 Corinthians 15:54 — resurrection language. Paul is expressing a preference, not describing an intermediate state. His own explicit teaching in 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 tells us WHEN being with the Lord happens: at the resurrection and catching up, not at death.

> "For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life." — 2 Corinthians 5:4

**11. Read Luke 23:43 and John 20:17.**

The Greek text of Luke 23:43 has no punctuation. The comma before "today" is an editorial decision in English Bibles. Two readings are equally valid in the Greek: (a) "I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise" or (b) "I say to you today, you will be with me in paradise." On Sunday morning — two days later — Jesus told Mary "I am not yet ascended to my Father" (John 20:17). If Jesus was not in paradise on Friday, reading (a) creates a contradiction. Reading (b) is consistent with all other passages: Jesus promises the thief on this day that he will be with Him in paradise — at the resurrection.

**12. Read Luke 16:19-31.**

This passage appears in a series of parables (Luke 15-16). The main teaching point is stated in verses 29 and 31: "They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them." The passage teaches about listening to God's Word, not about afterlife geography. "Abraham's bosom" as a location for the dead appears nowhere else in Scripture. A divided hades with conversations across a gulf is not described anywhere else in the Bible. Treating parabolic imagery as a literal map of the afterlife goes beyond what the text teaches.

**13. Read Revelation 6:9-11 and Genesis 4:10.**

Revelation 6 is an apocalyptic vision full of symbols — four horsemen, stars falling like figs, the sky rolling up like a scroll. The "souls under the altar" who cry out are part of this symbolic vision. Genesis 4:10 uses the same pattern: Abel's blood "crieth unto me from the ground." Abel's blood was not literally conscious and speaking — this is a figure of speech for injustice crying out for justice. The souls under the altar are told to "rest" — death and sleep vocabulary.

## Section D: When Do the Dead Receive Their Reward?

**14. Read 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17. What is the sequence Paul describes? When does "ever be with the Lord" begin?**

The sequence is: (1) the Lord descends, (2) the dead in Christ rise first, (3) the living are caught up together with them, (4) "and so shall we ever be with the Lord." Being with the Lord begins at the resurrection and Second Coming — not at death. Paul gives a specific sequence, and being with the Lord is the final step.

> "For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... and the dead in Christ shall rise first: then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord." — 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17

**15. Read Acts 2:29, 34. Has David ascended into the heavens?**

No. Peter directly states: "David is both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day" (v.29). And: "David is not ascended into the heavens" (v.34). David was a man after God's own heart — yet according to Peter's apostolic sermon, he has not gone to heaven. He is dead and buried.

**16. Read Hebrews 11:13, 39-40. Did the Old Testament heroes receive the promises when they died?**

No. "These all died in faith, not having received the promises" (v.13). "And these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise: God having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect" (v.39-40). The heroes of faith are waiting. They have not yet received what was promised. Their perfection awaits the resurrection.

**17. Read 2 Timothy 4:8. When is the crown of righteousness given?**

"At that day" — the day of Christ's appearing. Not at death, but at the Second Coming. Paul says the crown is laid up for him and for "all them also that love his appearing."

## Explicit or Inference? — Answers

**A. "The dead know not any thing."**
- **Explicit.** Ecclesiastes 9:5 directly states this.

**B. "When a believer dies, they immediately go to heaven as a conscious being."**
- **Inference.** No verse says this. Peter says David — a righteous man — "is not ascended into the heavens" (Acts 2:34). Hebrews says the heroes "died in faith, not having received the promises" (11:13). The claim requires adding the concept of immediate heavenly consciousness to texts that do not state it.

**C. "David is not ascended into the heavens."**
- **Explicit.** Acts 2:34 directly states this.

**D. "The parable of the rich man and Lazarus describes the literal geography of the afterlife."**
- **Inference.** Luke 16:19-31 is a parable in a series of parables. Its imagery (Abraham's bosom, conversations across a gulf) appears nowhere else in Scripture. Its own teaching point (v.29-31) is about hearing Moses and the prophets. The claim that it maps the literal afterlife requires treating parabolic imagery as doctrine — a framework the text does not provide.

**E. "The dead in Christ shall rise first: then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them... and so shall we ever be with the Lord."**
- **Explicit.** 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 directly states this.

## What the Bible Says vs. What People Say the Bible Teaches

| The Bible explicitly says... | People often claim the Bible teaches... |
|---|---|
| The dead know *nothing* (Ecc 9:5) | The dead are *conscious* in heaven or hell |
| Thoughts *perish* at death (Ps 146:4) | Thoughts *continue* in a spiritual realm |
| The dead go down into *silence* (Ps 115:17) | The dead are *praising God* in heaven |
| David is *not ascended* into the heavens (Acts 2:34) | The righteous dead go *immediately to heaven* |
| Being with the Lord begins at the *resurrection* (1 Thess 4:16-17) | Being with the Lord begins at *death* |
| The heroes died *not having received* the promises (Heb 11:13) | The heroes *received* their reward at death |
| Death is called *sleep* by 7+ authors | Death is a *transition* to conscious existence |
| The Greek of Luke 23:43 has *no punctuation* | A comma before "today" is in the *original text* |
