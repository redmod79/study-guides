# Study 4: State of the Dead — Conscious or Unconscious? — Advanced Study

*Word Studies, Genre Analysis, and the Alleged Conscious Intermediate State Passages*

---

> **Evidence Tiers**
>
> This series uses a three-tier evidence classification to distinguish what Scripture says from what people claim it teaches:
>
> 1. **Explicit (E)** — The text directly says this. You can point to a verse and quote it.
> 2. **Necessary Implication (N)** — No single verse says this word-for-word, but it unavoidably follows from what the text does say. No interpretation is needed — every reader, regardless of theological position, must agree.
> 3. **Inference (I)** — Someone claims the Bible teaches this, but no verse says it and no combination of verses forces it. Something must be *added* beyond what the text contains: a concept, a framework, or a choice between possible readings.
>
> The hierarchy matters: **Explicit > Necessary Implication > Inference.** An inference cannot override what the text explicitly states.

## Section A: The OT Death-State — Breadth and Consistency

1. At least eight OT authors across six or more books describe the state of the dead. Locate death-state statements from each of the following and note what each says about the dead's consciousness: (a) David — Ps 6:5; 30:9; 115:17, (b) Solomon — Ecc 9:5-6, 10, (c) the psalmist of Psalm 146 — Ps 146:4, (d) Heman the Ezrahite — Ps 88:10-12, (e) Job — Job 3:17-18; 14:10-12, 21, (f) Hezekiah — Isa 38:18-19. Do any of these authors describe the dead as conscious?

2. Read **Job 7:9-10**, **Job 10:21-22**, and **Job 17:13-16**. Job describes sheol as a land of _________, a house of _________, where the bed is in _________, and his companions are _________ and _________. Do these descriptions suggest activity and awareness, or cessation and darkness?

3. Read **Isaiah 26:14** and **Isaiah 26:19**. Verse 14 says the dead (rephaim) shall not live and shall not rise. Verse 19 says the dead shall live and those in the dust shall awake. How do these two verses relate to each other? (Hint: who are the subjects in each verse?)

4. Some argue that **Isaiah 14:9-10** describes conscious activity in sheol: the rephaim "speak," sheol is "moved." Read **Isaiah 14:4**. What does the text call this passage? What literary device does a *mashal* (taunt-poem) use when trees "rejoice" (v.8) and sheol is "stirred up" (v.9)?

## Section B: *Koimao* (G2837) — The Death-as-Sleep Word Study

5. The Greek verb *koimao* means "to put to sleep" or "to fall asleep." Trace its usage for death across the NT: **John 11:11**; **Acts 7:60**; **Acts 13:36**; **1 Cor 15:6, 18, 20, 51**; **1 Thess 4:13-15**; **2 Pet 3:4**. How many different authors use *koimao* for death? What does the breadth of usage suggest — occasional metaphor or standard terminology?

6. In **1 Corinthians 15:18**, Paul argues: "If Christ be not raised, then they also which are fallen asleep (*koimao*) in Christ are perished (*apollymi*)." If the dead were already consciously with Christ, would Paul's argument work? Why or why not?

7. Jesus says "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth (*koimao*); but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep" (**John 11:11**). The disciples misunderstood, thinking Jesus meant natural sleep (v.12-13). Jesus then said "plainly, Lazarus is dead" (v.14). What does Jesus' deliberate clarification tell us about how He intended the metaphor?

## Section C: Sheol (H7585) and Hades (G86) — Word Study

8. *Sheol* appears 67 times in the OT. Look up **Genesis 37:35**, **Psalm 9:17**, **Psalm 16:10**, and **Numbers 16:33**. Who goes to sheol in each passage? What does this tell you about whether sheol is divided into sections for the righteous and the wicked?

9. Read **Acts 2:27, 31** where Peter quotes **Psalm 16:10**. The Hebrew has *sheol*; Peter's Greek has *hades*. What does this direct quotation demonstrate about the relationship between sheol and hades?

10. Read **Revelation 20:13-14**. What happens to death and hades at the end? What does this tell you about whether hades is the final state of the dead?

11. Compile the didactic descriptions of sheol: **Ecclesiastes 9:10** (no work, device, knowledge, wisdom); **Psalm 6:5** (no remembrance, no thanks); **Psalm 115:17** (silence); **Job 10:21-22** (darkness); **Psalm 88:12** (forgetfulness). Does any didactic passage describe sheol as a place of conscious activity?

## Section D: The Alleged Conscious Intermediate State Passages — Detailed Examination

12. **2 Corinthians 5:1-8.** Read the full passage carefully. In verse 4, Paul says he does NOT want to be "unclothed" (*ekdysasthai*) but "clothed upon" (*ependysasthai*), "that mortality might be swallowed up of life." Compare this phrase with **1 Corinthians 15:54** — "death is swallowed up in victory." Both use the same verb (*katapino*) and context (mortality/death being overcome). What event is Paul describing — an intermediate disembodied state, or the resurrection? How does this affect the reading of verse 8?

13. **Philippians 1:23.** Paul desires "to depart and be with Christ, which is far better." Now read **Philippians 3:11** — "if by any means I might attain unto the resurrection of the dead." And **Philippians 3:20-21** — awaiting a Savior who "shall change our vile body." In the same letter, what does Paul identify as his hope? From the perspective of a sleeper who has no subjective experience of time, what is the relationship between departing (death) and being with Christ (resurrection)?

14. **Luke 23:43.** Greek manuscripts contained no punctuation — no commas, no spaces between words (scriptio continua). The comma before "today" was placed by later translators. Grammatically, *semeron* ("today") can modify either the preceding verb ("I say to you today") or the following clause ("today you will be with me"). Now consider: (a) **John 20:17** — Jesus has not yet ascended on Sunday morning. (b) **Acts 2:27, 31** — Jesus' soul was in hades, not paradise. (c) **Luke 23:46** — Jesus commits his spirit to the Father (quoting Ps 31:5). Which reading of Luke 23:43 is consistent with these passages?

15. **Luke 16:19-31.** Identify the genre indicators: (a) the passage follows a series of parables in Luke 15-16; (b) "a certain rich man" mirrors "a certain man had two sons" (15:11) and "a certain rich man had a steward" (16:1). Now identify elements that appear nowhere else in Scripture as literal afterlife descriptions: "Abraham's bosom," a compartmentalized hades with visible contact and conversation between sections. What is the stated teaching point in verses 29-31? How does treating parabolic imagery as literal afterlife geography relate to the genre?

16. **Revelation 6:9-11.** Read the passage in its context — the opening of the seven seals. The first four seals produce symbolic horsemen; the sixth seal produces falling stars and a rolling sky. In this symbolic vision, John sees "souls under the altar" who cry out. Read **Genesis 4:10** — Abel's blood "crieth unto me from the ground." Does blood cry literally? Now note: the souls are told to "rest" (*anapauomai*). What vocabulary domain does "rest" belong to — conscious activity, or death/sleep?

17. **1 Samuel 28:11-19.** An apparition identified as Samuel speaks through a medium. Read **Deuteronomy 18:10-12** — what does God say about necromancy? Read **1 Chronicles 10:13-14** — why did Saul die? The Chronicler says Saul "enquired of one that had a familiar spirit" — not that he enquired of Samuel. What does the Chronicler's assessment suggest about the identity of the apparition? Can doctrine about the state of the dead be derived from a condemned practice?

18. **Matthew 17:1-9.** Moses and Elijah appear with Jesus on the mount. Read **Matthew 17:9** — what does Jesus call this event (*horama*)? Read **2 Kings 2:11** — did Elijah die? Read **Jude 9** — what is referenced about Moses' body? Even if Moses and Elijah were literally present, one never died and the other may have been specially resurrected. Can unique cases establish what is normal for all the dead?

## Section E: The "Under the Sun" Objection

19. Some argue that Ecclesiastes' death-state statements (9:5-6, 10) are limited by the phrase "under the sun" and describe only an earthly perspective, not the actual state of the dead. Examine the claim: (a) In Ecclesiastes 9:5, does the phrase "under the sun" appear in verse 5 or in verse 6? (b) Are the death-state statements in **Psalm 146:4**, **Psalm 6:5**, **Psalm 115:17**, **Psalm 88:10-12**, **Job 14:10-12, 21**, and **Isaiah 38:18-19** in Ecclesiastes? Do these authors use the "under the sun" framework? (c) How many non-Ecclesiastes authors would need to be invalidated to maintain this objection?

## Section F: The Resurrection as the Biblical Hope

20. Read **Daniel 12:2** and **Daniel 12:13**. What do those who "sleep in the dust" eventually do? What is Daniel told to do until "the end of the days"?

21. Read **John 6:39, 40, 44, 54**. How many times does Jesus say he will raise believers "at the last day"? Does he ever describe raising them at the moment of death?

22. Read **John 11:24-25**. Martha says "I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day." Does Jesus correct her timing?
