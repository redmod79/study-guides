# Study 3: What Does "Death" Mean in the Bible? -- Answer Key

*The Origin, Nature, and Meaning of Death in Scripture*

---

> **Reminder from Study 1**
>
> We continue to distinguish between what the Bible **explicitly says** and what people **infer** from the Bible.
>
> - **Explicit:** You can point to a verse and quote it. The text directly says this.
> - **Inference:** Someone claims the Bible teaches something, but no single verse says it. They have drawn a conclusion that is not itself in the text.
>
> As you work through these questions, notice when you are reading what the text *says* versus what you have been *told* it means.

---

## Section A: The Origin of Death

**1. Read Genesis 2:17. What did God warn Adam would happen if he ate from the forbidden tree?**

God warned Adam "thou shalt surely die." The Hebrew construction is *mot tamut* -- an emphatic form meaning "dying you shall die." This same construction is used for judicial death penalties throughout the Torah (Exod 21:12, 15-17; Lev 20:2ff), where it invariably means actual physical death.

> "But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die." -- Genesis 2:17

**2. Read Genesis 3:19. After Adam sinned, how did God define the death that would come? What would happen to Adam's body?**

God defined death as returning to dust: "dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." This is the Bible's first explicit definition of death. It reverses the creation of Genesis 2:7, where God formed man from the dust.

> "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." -- Genesis 3:19

**3. Read Genesis 3:22-24. Why did God bar Adam from the tree of life? What would the tree have given him?**

God barred Adam from the tree of life "lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever." The tree of life would have given him the ability to live forever. If man already possessed an immortal component that could not die, barring him from the tree would have been pointless.

**4. Read Romans 5:12. How did death enter the world? Is death limited to certain people, or does it pass upon all?**

Death entered the world through one man's sin. It is universal: "death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned." Paul identifies Adam's sin as the origin point of death for the entire human race.

> "Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned." -- Romans 5:12

## Section B: What Happens at Death?

**5. Read Ecclesiastes 12:7. What happens to the body at death? What happens to the spirit?**

The dust (body) returns to the earth, and the spirit (*ruach*) returns to God who gave it. This reverses the creation formula of Genesis 2:7. Creation was dust + breath = living soul. Death is dust returns + spirit returns = the living soul ceases.

> "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it." -- Ecclesiastes 12:7

**6. Read Psalm 146:4. When a person's breath departs, what happens to their thoughts? Does the text say this is gradual or immediate?**

Their thoughts perish "in that very day." The cessation of thought is immediate. The text does not describe a transition to another mode of existence -- it describes the end of all cognitive activity at the point of death.

> "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish." -- Psalm 146:4

**7. Read Ecclesiastes 9:5-6. What do the dead know? What has happened to their love, hatred, and envy?**

The dead know nothing. "The dead know not any thing." Their memory is forgotten, and "their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished." Every capacity -- knowledge, emotion, memory -- has ceased.

> "For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten. Also their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished." -- Ecclesiastes 9:5-6

**8. Read Psalm 115:17 and Psalm 6:5. Do the dead praise God? Is there remembrance of God in death?**

No. The dead go down into silence (Ps 115:17). There is no remembrance of God in death and no giving of thanks in the grave (Ps 6:5). Multiple biblical authors say the same thing.

> "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence." -- Psalm 115:17

> "For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?" -- Psalm 6:5

**9. Read Job 14:21. Does a dead man know what happens to his children?**

No. "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not of them." The dead have no awareness of events among the living.

## Section C: Death as Sleep

**10. Read John 11:11-14. What word did Jesus use for Lazarus' condition? What did He then clarify?**

Jesus said "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth." When the disciples misunderstood, He clarified plainly: "Lazarus is dead." Jesus Himself equated death with sleep.

> "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep... Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead." -- John 11:11, 14

**11. Read Acts 7:60 and 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14. What term did Luke and Paul use for the death of believers?**

Both use the language of sleep. Stephen "fell asleep" (Acts 7:60). Paul refers to believers who have died as "them which are asleep" (1 Thess 4:13). At least seven biblical authors use this metaphor for death.

**12. Read Daniel 12:2. Where are those who "sleep"? What will happen to them?**

They sleep "in the dust of the earth." They shall awake -- "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt." The dead are in the dust (not in heaven or hell), and the awakening is future (at the resurrection).

> "And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt." -- Daniel 12:2

## Section D: Death, Sin, and the Wages of Sin

**13. Read Romans 6:23. What is the wage (payment) of sin? What is the gift of God? Are these the same thing, or opposites?**

The wages of sin is death. The gift of God is eternal life. These are presented as opposites -- exhaustive alternatives with no third option. Death is contrasted with life, not with another form of life.

> "For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord." -- Romans 6:23

**14. Read Ezekiel 18:4, 20. What happens to the soul that sins?**

It dies. "The soul that sinneth, it shall die." God Himself says the *nephesh* (soul) is subject to death. This is a direct statement from God, not a philosophical inference.

> "The soul that sinneth, it shall die." -- Ezekiel 18:20

**15. Read Romans 8:6. What does Paul say is the result of being carnally minded? What is the result of being spiritually minded?**

"To be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." Paul presents death and life as a binary pair -- two outcomes, no third alternative.

**16. Read Deuteronomy 30:15, 19. What two choices does God set before His people? How many options are there?**

God sets before them "life and death, blessing and cursing." There are two options, not three. God urges: "choose life." The framework is binary: life or death.

> "I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil... I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life." -- Deuteronomy 30:15, 19

## Section E: The Second Death

**17. Read Revelation 20:14. What is the "second death" identified with?**

The second death is explicitly identified with the lake of fire. "Death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death." The word used for "death" here is *thanatos* -- the same Greek word used for death throughout the New Testament.

> "And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death." -- Revelation 20:14

**18. Read Revelation 21:8. Who has their part in the lake of fire? What does the text call this?**

The fearful, unbelieving, abominable, murderers, sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and liars have their part in the lake of fire. The text calls this "the second death." It does not call it "eternal torment" or "eternal separation" -- it calls it *death*.

> "But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and whoremongers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death." -- Revelation 21:8

**19. Read Revelation 20:6. Over whom does the second death have no power?**

Those who have part in the first resurrection. "On such the second death hath no power." Believers are exempt from the second death through the resurrection.

**20. Read 1 Corinthians 15:26 and Revelation 21:4. What ultimately happens to death itself?**

Death is destroyed. Paul calls it "the last enemy that shall be destroyed" (1 Cor 15:26). Revelation says "there shall be no more death" (Rev 21:4). Death is not eternal -- it is abolished.

## Explicit or Inference? -- Answers

**A. "Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return."**
- **Explicit.** Genesis 3:19 directly states this. God Himself spoke these words to Adam as the definition of the death that would come.

**B. "Death means the separation of the soul from the body."**
- **Inference.** No verse says this. James 2:26 says "the body without the spirit is dead," but that describes what a dead body *is*, not what death *means*. The text does not say the spirit continues to live consciously after departing. Psalm 146:4 says thoughts perish when breath departs.

**C. "The wages of sin is death."**
- **Explicit.** Romans 6:23 directly states this.

**D. "Death means separation from God."**
- **Inference.** No verse says "death means separation from God." Ephesians 4:18 says the unsaved are "alienated from the life of God," but this describes a condition of the *living*, not a definition of death. Genesis 3:19, not Ephesians 4:18, provides the Bible's definition of death.

**E. "The second death is the lake of fire."**
- **Explicit.** Revelation 20:14 directly states: "This is the second death." Revelation 21:8 repeats it: "which is the second death."

## What the Bible Says vs. What People Say the Bible Teaches

| The Bible explicitly says... | People often claim the Bible teaches... |
|---|---|
| Death is returning to dust (Gen 3:19) | Death is "separation of soul from body" |
| The dead know nothing (Ecc 9:5) | The dead are conscious in another realm |
| Thoughts perish at death (Ps 146:4) | Thoughts continue after death |
| The wages of sin is *death* (Rom 6:23) | The wages of sin is *eternal torment* |
| Death and life are the two alternatives (Deut 30:19) | Death is actually a different form of life |
| The second death is the lake of fire (Rev 20:14) | The second death is eternal conscious torment |
| The soul that sins *dies* (Ezek 18:4) | The soul *cannot* die |
